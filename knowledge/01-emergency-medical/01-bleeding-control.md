# Controlling Severe Bleeding

Severe bleeding is the most preventable cause of trauma death. An adult can bleed to death in under 5 minutes from a major artery.

## Recognize life-threatening bleeding

- Spurting or steady pouring blood
- Blood pooling on the ground
- Clothing soaked with blood
- Bandages soaking through
- Amputation or partial amputation
- Bleeding with confusion or unconsciousness

## Step 1: Direct pressure

1. Expose the wound (cut or tear clothing).
2. Place the cleanest cloth available directly on the wound.
3. Press hard with both hands, straight arms, body weight over the wound.
4. Hold for at least 3–5 minutes (10 is better) without peeking.
5. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top; do not remove the first layer.

## Step 2: Wound packing (junctional and deep wounds)

For wounds in the groin, armpit, or neck, or deep gashes where a tourniquet cannot be used:

1. Find the source of bleeding at the bottom of the wound.
2. Pack gauze (or the cleanest cloth strips) tightly into the wound, pushing with fingers until the cavity is full.
3. Hold firm pressure on top for at least 3 minutes (hemostatic gauze) or 10 minutes (plain gauze).
4. Wrap with a pressure bandage.

Never pack chest or abdominal wounds — cover them instead.

## Step 3: Tourniquet (limbs)

Use for arm or leg bleeding that is life-threatening or not controlled by pressure.

1. Place 2–3 inches (5–8 cm) above the wound, never directly on a joint. If the wound location is unclear, go "high and tight" at the top of the limb.
2. Tighten until the bleeding stops **and** the pulse below is gone. It will hurt badly. That is expected.
3. If bleeding continues, place a second tourniquet just above the first.
4. Write the time on the tourniquet or the casualty's forehead ("TK 14:35").
5. Leave it on. Removing or converting a tourniquet is a skilled decision; limbs typically tolerate about 2 hours. If no care will ever be reachable, trained responders may attempt conversion to a pressure dressing after shock is treated, watching closely for re-bleeding.

### Improvised tourniquet

- Band: a cloth strip 1.5–2 inches (4–5 cm) wide (triangular bandage, shirt strip). Never wire, string, or zip-ties — they cut tissue and fail.
- Windlass: a strong stick, tent stake, or wrench.
- Tie the band around the limb, place the stick on the knot, tie another knot over it, twist until bleeding stops, then lash the stick in place so it cannot unwind.

## Shock management after bleeding control

- Lay flat, keep warm (insulate from the ground), reassure.
- Raise legs about 12 inches if there is no leg, pelvis, or head injury.
- Small sips of water or oral rehydration solution only if fully alert, not vomiting, and no abdominal injury.

## Nosebleeds

Sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing. Cold compress over the bridge. Seek care if it lasts over 30 minutes or follows head trauma.

## Internal bleeding signs

Rigid or swelling abdomen, bruising on the flank or around the navel, vomiting blood or coffee-ground material, black tarry stools, rising pulse with pale clammy skin and no visible bleeding. Field treatment is limited to shock care; surgical care is the only real treatment.
