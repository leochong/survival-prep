# Burn Treatment

## Immediate steps

1. Stop the burning: stop, drop, roll; remove smoldering clothing and jewelry (rings before swelling sets in).
2. Cool with cool (not ice-cold) clean running water for 20 minutes. Still useful up to 3 hours after the burn. No ice, butter, or oils.
3. For large burns, cool only briefly and then keep the person warm — big burns cause hypothermia.
4. Cover loosely with clean plastic wrap or a clean, dry, non-fluffy cloth.

## Depth

- **Superficial (1st degree):** red, painful, no blisters (like sunburn). Heals in about a week.
- **Partial thickness (2nd degree):** blisters, wet, very painful. Heals in 2–3 weeks if kept clean.
- **Full thickness (3rd degree):** white, leathery, brown or charred, may be painless. Will not heal on its own beyond small areas.

## Size: rule of nines (adults)

Head 9%, each arm 9%, front of trunk 18%, back of trunk 18%, each leg 18%, groin 1%. The patient's own palm with fingers ≈ 1% of body surface.

## Serious burns — evacuate if any care exists

- Partial or full thickness over 10% (adult) or 5% (child/elderly)
- Burns of face, hands, feet, genitals, or across joints
- Circumferential burns (all the way around a limb or chest)
- Electrical, chemical, or inhalation burns

## Fluid replacement (oral)

Large burns leak fluid. If no IV is possible and the person is alert and not vomiting, give oral rehydration solution (see *Dehydration and Oral Rehydration*) steadily. Target urine output of about 30–50 mL per hour for an adult (pale yellow urine, urinating every 2–3 hours).

## Blisters and dressings

- Leave small blisters intact. Large, tense, or broken blisters may be drained or trimmed with sterile technique.
- Wash gently daily with clean water and mild soap.
- Apply a thin layer of antibiotic ointment, honey, or petroleum jelly and a non-stick dressing. Change daily.
- Watch for infection: increasing pain, spreading redness, fever, green discharge, foul odor.

## Airway burns

Singed nose hairs, soot around the mouth, hoarse voice, coughing soot, or burns in an enclosed space suggest inhalation injury. The airway can swell shut within hours. Keep upright and monitor closely; this needs advanced care.

## Chemical burns

Brush off dry chemicals first, then flush with large amounts of water for at least 20 minutes (alkalis like lye or quicklime: 60 minutes or more — they penetrate deeper). Eyes: flush continuously, holding the lids open, for at least 20 minutes.

## Electrical burns

Make sure the power is off before touching. Small surface burns can hide deep damage. Check for an irregular pulse; start CPR if needed.
