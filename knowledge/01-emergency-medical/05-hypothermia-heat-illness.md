# Cold and Heat Emergencies

## Hypothermia

Core temperature below 95°F (35°C). Can happen at 50°F (10°C) when wet and windy.

### Stages

- **Mild:** shivering, clumsy hands, "the umbles" (stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles).
- **Moderate:** violent then decreasing shivering, confusion, poor judgment, slurred speech.
- **Severe:** shivering stops, very drowsy to unconscious, slow pulse and breathing. Paradoxical undressing may occur.

### Treatment

1. Get out of wind, rain, and off cold ground. Insulate from the ground first.
2. Remove wet clothing (cut it off if needed to avoid excess movement) and replace with dry layers.
3. **Hypothermia wrap ("burrito"):** vapor barrier (plastic sheet, tarp) → sleeping bags/blankets → outer vapor barrier. Cover the head, leave the face exposed.
4. Heat sources: warm (not scalding) water bottles wrapped in cloth at the armpits, chest, and back. Skin-to-skin in a sleeping bag works for mild cases.
5. If alert and able to swallow: warm sweet drinks and high-calorie food. Sugar fuels shivering.
6. Severe hypothermia: handle **very gently** (rough movement can trigger cardiac arrest), keep horizontal, do not let them walk or rub limbs. Check pulse for a full 60 seconds before starting CPR.

"No one is dead until warm and dead" applies to severe hypothermia when resources allow prolonged effort.

## Frostbite

Skin is white, waxy, hard, numb.

- Prevent further cold injury; protect the area.
- **Do not thaw if there is any chance it will refreeze** — refreezing causes far worse damage. Walking out on frozen feet is better than thawing them and then freezing again.
- Rewarm in water at 98.6–102°F (37–39°C) (comfortable to an uninjured hand) for 15–30 minutes until tissue is soft and red/purple. It will be very painful; give ibuprofen if available.
- Never rub with snow or massage. Do not pop blisters. Pad between fingers/toes with dry gauze. Protect for weeks; dead tissue declares itself over 1–3 months.

## Trench / immersion foot

Feet wet and cold (not frozen) for many hours: numb, swollen, then painful. Prevent by drying feet nightly, rotating dry socks, and airing feet. Treat by warming, drying, elevating.

## Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness, normal mental status.

- Move to shade, lie down, loosen clothing, raise legs.
- Fan and wet the skin.
- Drink oral rehydration solution or water with salty snacks.
- Rest the remainder of the day.

## Heat stroke — EMERGENCY

Hot skin (may still be sweating) **plus altered mental status**: confusion, strange behavior, seizure, collapse. Core temperature above 104°F (40°C).

**Cool immediately, before anything else:**
1. Best: immerse the body to the neck in cold water (creek, tub, tarp-lined pit) with someone holding the head.
2. Otherwise: soak with water and fan aggressively; ice or cold packs to the neck, armpits, groin, and cheeks/palms/soles; rotate wet cold towels.
3. Stop active cooling when the person becomes clearly alert or feels cool, to avoid overshooting into hypothermia.
4. Do not give fluids by mouth unless fully alert.

## Prevention

- **Cold:** layers (wicking, insulating, windproof/waterproof), avoid cotton, eat often, stay dry, buddy-check for "umbles".
- **Heat:** acclimatize over 7–14 days, work in early morning/evening, drink regularly, eat salt, wear loose light clothing and a wide-brimmed hat, monitor urine color.
