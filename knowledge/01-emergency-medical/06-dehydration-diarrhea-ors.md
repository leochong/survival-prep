# Dehydration, Diarrhea, and Oral Rehydration Solution

After disasters, diarrheal disease from contaminated water is one of the biggest killers, especially of young children and the elderly. Oral rehydration solution (ORS) saves most of these lives.

## Signs of dehydration

- **Some dehydration:** thirsty, restless/irritable, dry mouth, dark urine, sunken eyes, skin pinch returns slowly.
- **Severe:** lethargic or unconscious, unable to drink, very sunken eyes, skin pinch returns very slowly (more than 2 seconds), little or no urine, fast weak pulse. In infants: sunken soft spot on the head.

## Homemade ORS (WHO-recommended recipe)

- **1 liter (about 1 quart)** clean (boiled and cooled or disinfected) water
- **6 level teaspoons** sugar
- **½ level teaspoon** salt

Stir until dissolved. It should taste no saltier than tears. Make fresh daily; discard after 24 hours.

Cereal-based alternative: boil ½–1 cup cooked rice flour (or well-cooked mashed rice) in 1 liter of water with ½ teaspoon salt into a thin, drinkable gruel. Often reduces stool volume better than sugar ORS.

Add potassium if possible: give mashed banana, coconut water, or orange/lemon juice alongside ORS.

## How much to give

- **Child under 2:** ¼–½ cup (50–100 mL) after each loose stool.
- **Child 2–10:** ½–1 cup (100–200 mL) after each loose stool.
- **Older children and adults:** as much as they want; at least 2–3 liters a day plus losses.
- If vomiting: wait 10 minutes, then give slowly — a teaspoon every 1–2 minutes. Most vomiting children still absorb ORS this way.
- Keep breastfeeding infants throughout. Continue feeding; do not starve diarrhea.

Zinc supplements (children 10–20 mg daily for 10–14 days), if available, shorten childhood diarrhea.

## Danger signs — seek any care available

- Blood in stool with fever (dysentery — may need antibiotics)
- Rice-water stools in large volumes (possible cholera — needs aggressive rehydration, often liters per hour)
- Unable to drink or keep anything down
- Lethargy, seizures, or no urine for 8+ hours (6 in infants)
- Diarrhea lasting more than 14 days

## Prevention

- Treat all drinking water (see *Water Purification*).
- Wash hands with soap after toilet use and before handling food.
- Latrines at least 200 feet (60 m) from water sources and downhill.
- Cook food thoroughly; eat it hot; protect from flies.
- Isolate sick people's bedding, waste, and dishes.
