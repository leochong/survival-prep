# CPR, Choking, and Airway Management

## Recovery position

For an unconscious person who is breathing normally: roll onto their side, top knee bent forward to stop rolling, head tilted back with the mouth pointing down so fluids drain. Check breathing continually.

## Adult CPR

1. Check responsiveness: tap and shout.
2. Check breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is **not** normal breathing.
3. If not breathing: start compressions.
   - Heel of hand in the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone), other hand on top.
   - Push hard and fast: **2–2.4 inches (5–6 cm) deep, 100–120 per minute** (the beat of "Stayin' Alive").
   - Let the chest fully recoil between compressions.
4. If trained and willing: **30 compressions : 2 breaths.** Head-tilt/chin-lift, pinch nose, one-second breaths that make the chest rise.
5. Hands-only CPR (no breaths) is acceptable for adults who collapse suddenly.
6. Swap compressors every 2 minutes to avoid fatigue.

### Children and infants

- **Child (1 year–puberty):** one or two hands, depth about 2 inches (5 cm). With two rescuers, 15:2.
- **Infant:** two fingers (or two thumbs encircling the chest) just below the nipple line, depth about 1.5 inches (4 cm).
- In children and drowning victims, breathing problems usually cause the arrest — give breaths. For drowning, start with 5 rescue breaths.

### When to stop (no EMS)

Without defibrillation and advanced care, prolonged CPR rarely restores circulation except in hypothermia, drowning in cold water, lightning strikes, and drug overdoses. Reasonable stopping points: the person revives, you are exhausted, the scene becomes unsafe, or about 30 minutes of effort has produced no response (longer for cold-water drowning/hypothermia).

### Lightning

Multiple casualties: treat those who appear dead **first** (reverse triage) — lightning victims in arrest often recover with CPR and rescue breathing, while those who are moving will usually survive.

## Choking

**Mild** (can cough, speak): encourage coughing; stay with them.

**Severe** (cannot breathe, speak, or cough; clutching throat):
- **Adult/child:** 5 back blows between the shoulder blades, then 5 abdominal thrusts (fist above navel, quick inward-upward thrusts). Repeat until cleared or unconscious.
- **Pregnant or obese:** chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.
- **Infant:** face down along your forearm, head low: 5 back blows; flip, 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. Never abdominal thrusts on infants.
- **Self:** thrust your abdomen against a chair back or railing.
- If they go unconscious: lower to the ground, start CPR; look in the mouth before breaths and remove any visible object (no blind finger sweeps).

## Opioid overdose

Pinpoint pupils, very slow or no breathing, unresponsive. Give naloxone (nasal spray) if available, rescue breaths, recovery position. Naloxone wears off in 30–90 minutes; overdose can return. Stock naloxone in medical kits.

## Anaphylaxis

Hives, swelling of face/lips/tongue, wheezing, difficulty breathing, vomiting, collapse after a sting, food, or medication. Give an epinephrine auto-injector into the outer thigh immediately; a second dose after 5–15 minutes if no improvement. Antihistamines (diphenhydramine) help itching/hives but do not replace epinephrine. Lay flat (or sitting if breathing is hard).
