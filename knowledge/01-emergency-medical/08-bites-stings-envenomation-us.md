# Venomous Bites and Stings in the United States

## Venomous snakes by region

- **Pit vipers (rattlesnakes, copperheads, cottonmouths/water moccasins):** triangular head, heat-sensing pits between eye and nostril, vertical pupils. Rattlesnakes occur in nearly every contiguous state; diversity is highest in the Southwest. Copperheads: East, Southeast, south Midwest, Texas. Cottonmouths: Southeast wetlands to east Texas and southern Illinois.
- **Coral snakes:** Southeast (Florida, coastal plain to east Texas) and Arizona/New Mexico. Red, yellow, and black bands with red touching yellow in US species (the rhyme is unreliable elsewhere — treat any banded snake with caution).
- **Alaska and Hawaii:** no native land snakes of medical concern.

### Pit viper bite: what to do

1. Move away from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it; photograph from a distance if easy.
2. Keep the victim calm and still. Remove rings, watches, tight clothing before swelling.
3. Splint the limb loosely and keep it at about heart level.
4. Mark the swelling edge with a pen every 15–30 minutes with the time.
5. Evacuate toward antivenom — carry the victim if possible.

**Do NOT:** apply a tourniquet, cut, suck, use suction devices, apply ice, give alcohol, or use electric shock. For pit vipers, do not use pressure-immobilization wraps (they concentrate tissue damage).

About 20–25% of pit viper bites are "dry" (no venom). Signs of envenomation: rapidly spreading swelling and pain, bruising, metallic taste, tingling around mouth, nausea, weakness, bleeding gums.

### Coral snake bite

Effects may be delayed 12 hours or more, then cause weakness, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, and breathing failure. A pressure-immobilization wrap (snug elastic bandage over the whole limb, like for a sprain, plus splint) is reasonable for coral snakes. Be prepared for rescue breathing.

## Spiders

- **Black widow** (all lower 48, most common in the South and West): shiny black with a red hourglass under the abdomen. Bite causes severe muscle cramping, abdominal rigidity, sweating, high blood pressure within hours. Clean the bite, apply cold, pain control. Usually survivable in healthy adults; dangerous for small children and the elderly.
- **Brown recluse** (South-Central and Midwest: Missouri, Kansas, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Kentucky and surroundings): brown with a violin-shaped mark, six eyes. Bites can ulcerate over days. Clean, cool, elevate, keep immobile; most heal without surgery. Many "recluse bites" outside that range are actually skin infections (MRSA) — watch for spreading infection.

## Scorpions

Arizona bark scorpion (Arizona, parts of New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, California) is the only US species of medical concern. Immediate burning pain, numbness, muscle twitching, roving eye movements in children. Cold compress, pain relief, keep still; children may need antivenom and airway support.

## Bees, wasps, fire ants

Scrape out bee stingers quickly (any method). Cold compress. Watch closely for anaphylaxis (see *CPR, Choking, and Airway*). Fire ants (Southeast, Texas, California): pustules — do not open them; keep clean.

## Ticks and tick-borne disease

- **Lyme disease** (Northeast, upper Midwest, mid-Atlantic, northern California): expanding "bull's-eye" rash 3–30 days after bite, fever, joint pain. Doxycycline is the standard treatment.
- **Rocky Mountain spotted fever** (mostly Southeast and south-central, despite its name): fever, headache, then a spotted rash on wrists/ankles spreading inward. Can be fatal in days — treat early.
- **Removal:** grasp the tick close to the skin with fine tweezers and pull steadily straight out. Do not twist, burn, or smother with petroleum jelly. Clean the site; note the date.
- **Prevention:** long pants tucked into socks, permethrin-treated clothing, daily full-body tick checks.

## Marine (coastal, Hawaii)

- **Jellyfish:** rinse with seawater (not fresh water); for box jellyfish in Hawaii, vinegar. Remove tentacles with a gloved hand. Hot-water immersion (113°F/45°C, as hot as tolerable) for 20–40 minutes relieves many stings.
- **Stingray / spiny fish:** soak in hot water (as hot as tolerable without burning) for 30–90 minutes to break down the venom; irrigate and watch for infection.
