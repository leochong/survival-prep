# Emergency Childbirth Without a Hospital

Most births are normal and the mother's body does the work. Your job: keep things clean, calm, and warm, and recognize danger signs.

## Signs labor is advanced

Contractions 2–3 minutes apart and lasting a minute, strong urge to push or "have a bowel movement", bulging of the perineum, baby's head visible (crowning).

## Preparation

Clean surface with clean sheets/towels, plastic underneath; soap and water; boiled string or shoelaces (two pieces) and a boiled or new razor/scissors for the cord; warm blankets for the baby; a bowl for the placenta.

## Delivery

1. Wash hands thoroughly; gloves if available.
2. Mother in a comfortable position — semi-sitting, on hands and knees, or side-lying.
3. As the head crowns, support it gently with a hand to prevent sudden explosive delivery; encourage the mother to pant rather than push hard.
4. Once the head is out, feel for the cord around the neck. If loose, slip it over the head. If tight, deliver the baby through it (the "somersault") or, if impossible, clamp in two places and cut.
5. The shoulders usually follow with the next contraction: gently guide the head downward to free the top shoulder, then upward for the bottom shoulder. Do not pull hard.
6. The baby will be slippery — hold securely.

## Newborn care (first minutes)

1. Dry vigorously with a warm towel, remove wet towel, place skin-to-skin on the mother's chest, cover both.
2. Most babies cry within 30 seconds. If not breathing: rub the back, flick the soles; clear the mouth then nose. If still not breathing after 1 minute, give gentle rescue breaths (cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth, small puffs) 40 per minute.
3. Keep the baby warm — newborns lose heat dangerously fast.

## Cord

Wait at least 1–3 minutes (until the cord stops pulsing) before tying. Tie tightly about 2 fingers' width (4 cm) from the baby's belly and again 2 inches farther out; cut between the ties with a sterile blade. Keep the stump clean and dry.

## Placenta

Usually delivers within 30 minutes. Do not pull on the cord. Encourage breastfeeding — this releases oxytocin, contracting the uterus. Check that the placenta looks complete; retained pieces cause bleeding and infection.

## Postpartum bleeding (most important danger)

- After the placenta is out, feel the uterus through the belly below the navel: it should be firm like a grapefruit.
- If soft or bleeding is heavy: **massage firmly** in circles until firm; keep the baby nursing; empty the bladder; lay mother flat with legs raised; give fluids.
- If bleeding continues: bimanual compression (trained) or press a fist hard into the lower belly above the pubic bone.
- If misoprostol is in the kit, it is a WHO-listed drug for postpartum hemorrhage; follow its labeled dosing.

## Danger signs — get skilled help at any cost

Baby not head-first (breech, arm, or cord coming first), labor over 12 hours of strong contractions with no progress, heavy bleeding before birth, seizures (eclampsia), fever, foul-smelling discharge, a baby that is not breathing well, not feeding, too cold, or turns yellow within the first day.

If the cord comes out first (prolapse): mother on hands and knees with hips high, hold the presenting part off the cord with gloved fingers, keep the cord moist and warm, and seek help immediately.
