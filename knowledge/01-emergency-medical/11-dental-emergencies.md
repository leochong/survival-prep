# Dental Emergencies and Oral Hygiene

Dental infections can spread to the face, neck, and brain. Prevention is far easier than treatment.

## Prevention

- Brush twice daily. Improvised toothbrush: chew the end of a green twig (dogwood, birch, willow) into fibers.
- Tooth powder: baking soda, or fine salt. Floss with thread or thin plant fiber.
- Limit sugar; rinse with water after eating.

## Toothache

- Rinse with warm salt water (½ teaspoon salt per cup).
- Clean out the cavity gently with a toothpick and rinse.
- **Clove oil** (eugenol): a drop on cotton placed in the cavity numbs pain. Whole cloves chewed near the tooth work too. Do not soak the gums repeatedly — it irritates tissue.
- Ibuprofen works better than acetaminophen for dental pain; together they are better still.
- Temporary filling: commercial temporary filling material; in a pinch, softened candle wax or sugar-free gum to keep food out.

## Dental abscess

Swelling of gum or face, throbbing pain, pus, fever. Warm salt-water rinses many times a day. A swollen gum "pimple" may drain if lanced with a sterile needle. Antibiotics and drainage or extraction are the definitive treatment. **Emergency:** swelling under the tongue or jaw, difficulty swallowing or breathing — spreading infection that can close the airway.

## Knocked-out permanent tooth

1. Pick it up by the crown, not the root.
2. If dirty, rinse briefly in milk or saline (do not scrub).
3. Reinsert into the socket immediately if possible and have the person bite on cloth. Best within 30 minutes.
4. Otherwise store in milk, saliva (inside the cheek), or saline. Not plain water.
5. Splint to neighbors with soft wax/gum for 1–2 weeks; soft food.

Do not reinsert baby teeth.

## Broken tooth / lost filling

Rinse, cover sharp edges with wax or gum, avoid chewing on that side. Pain on exposure to cold/air usually means exposed dentin; exposed red/pink pulp is likely to become infected.

## Extraction

Only as a last resort, for a severely infected or broken tooth, by the most skilled person available with proper forceps. Risks: broken roots, bleeding, jaw fracture, spreading infection. After extraction, bite on gauze for 30–60 minutes; a damp black-tea bag helps clotting. No spitting, straws, or smoking for 2 days (dry socket).
