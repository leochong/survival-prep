# Wild Mushroom Safety

Mushrooms offer few calories (mostly water and fiber) — **in a survival situation, they are rarely worth the risk.** There is no universal test for mushroom edibility; the Universal Edibility Test does not work for fungi.

## The deadly ones (US)

- **Death cap (*Amanita phalloides*):** spreading in California, Pacific Northwest, and the Northeast, often under oaks and introduced trees. Greenish-yellow to bronze cap, white gills, ring on stem, sac-like cup (volva) at the base, often buried — dig up the whole mushroom.
- **Destroying angels (*Amanita bisporigera, A. ocreata*, etc.):** pure white, gills white, ring, volva. Eastern US (*bisporigera*) and California (*ocreata*).
- **Deadly galerina (*Galerina marginata*):** small brown mushrooms on rotting wood; same amatoxins. Confused with honey mushrooms.
- **False morels (*Gyromitra*):** brain-like, wrinkled, lobed cap; when sliced lengthwise the inside is chambered or cottony. **True morels are completely hollow** from cap tip through the stem, with the cap attached directly to the stem.
- **Webcaps (*Cortinarius*):** kidney failure, delayed days to weeks.

## Relatively distinctive edibles (still require in-person confirmation)

- **Morels (*Morchella*):** spring, pitted honeycomb cap, fully hollow inside. Always cook.
- **Chicken of the woods (*Laetiporus*):** bright orange/yellow shelves on wood; no gills. Some people react; avoid those growing on conifers, eucalyptus, or yew.
- **Giant puffball (*Calvatia gigantea*):** large white ball; must be pure white and uniform inside when sliced. Any outline of a cap/gills inside → it's a young Amanita.
- **Chanterelles (*Cantharellus*):** have blunt, forking false gills running down the stem; lookalike jack-o'-lantern has true sharp gills and grows in clusters on wood.
- **Oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus*):** on dead hardwood, shelf-like, gills running down a short stem.

## Rules

1. Never eat a mushroom based on an app, photo, or written description alone.
2. Never eat any small brown mushroom or any white-gilled mushroom with a ring and cup.
3. Cook all wild mushrooms. Eat a small amount the first time; save an uncooked sample in the refrigerator/cool place in case of poisoning.
4. Don't eat mushrooms that are old, slimy, or bug-infested.

## Poisoning symptoms timeline

- **Within 2 hours:** usually GI upset from less dangerous species (still treat dehydration).
- **6–24 hours later:** amatoxin poisoning (death cap etc.) — vomiting, diarrhea, an apparent improvement, then liver failure days 3–5. Needs hospital care urgently. Give oral rehydration; activated charcoal (repeated doses) is a recognized supportive measure.
