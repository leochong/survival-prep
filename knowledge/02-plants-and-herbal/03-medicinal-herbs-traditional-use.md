# Medicinal Herbs — Traditional Use and Evidence

Evidence ratings: **[Good]** clinical trial support; **[Some]** limited or mixed human data; **[Traditional]** mainly folk use.

## Preparation methods

- **Infusion (tea):** pour boiling water over leaves/flowers, cover, steep 10–15 minutes. Typically 1–2 teaspoons dried herb per cup.
- **Decoction:** simmer roots, bark, or seeds 20–30 minutes.
- **Poultice:** crushed or chewed fresh plant applied directly to skin, covered with cloth.
- **Infused oil / salve:** dried herb covered with oil, kept warm (not hot) for several hours or in sun for 2–4 weeks, strained; melt in beeswax (about 1 part wax to 4–5 parts oil) for a salve.
- **Tincture:** herb soaked in 80–100 proof alcohol for 4–6 weeks, strained.

## Wound and skin herbs

- **Plantain (*Plantago major/lanceolata*)** — nationwide "weed" with parallel leaf veins. Chewed leaf poultice for insect bites, stings, minor wounds, splinters. **[Traditional/Some]** Young leaves edible.
- **Yarrow (*Achillea millefolium*)** — feathery leaves, flat white flower clusters. Crushed leaf on minor cuts to slow bleeding (folk name "soldier's woundwort"). **[Traditional]** Do not confuse with poison hemlock/carrot family (yarrow leaves are finely feathered and fragrant, stems hairy, flowers have distinct ray florets). Avoid in pregnancy.
- **Jewelweed (*Impatiens capensis*)** — wet woods in the East; orange spotted flowers, "touch-me-not" exploding seed pods. Crushed stem juice traditionally applied after poison ivy exposure. **[Some/mixed]**
- **Calendula** (garden) — infused oil or salve for minor wounds and rashes. **[Some]**
- **Comfrey (*Symphytum*)** — external poultice for bruises and sprains **[Some]**. **Do not take internally** (pyrrolizidine alkaloids damage the liver) and do not use on deep or dirty wounds (can close skin over infection).
- **Pine/spruce resin** — traditional antiseptic wound dressing and drawing salve. **[Traditional/Some]**
- **Honey** — wound dressing with good evidence **[Good]** (see *Wound Care*).
- **Aloe** (Southwest, warm-region gardens) — gel for minor burns and sunburn **[Some]**.

## Pain and fever

- **Willow bark (*Salix*)** — contains salicin (aspirin precursor). Decoct inner bark. **[Some]** for back pain. Same cautions as aspirin: not for children, people on blood thinners, with ulcers, or aspirin allergy. Slower onset and weaker than aspirin.
- **Feverfew** — migraine prevention **[Some]**; not in pregnancy.

## Colds, cough, immunity

- **Elderberry (*Sambucus nigra/canadensis*)** — cooked berry syrup may shorten flu/cold duration **[Some]**. Raw berries, stems, leaves, and roots contain cyanogenic compounds — cook berries and strain seeds.
- **Echinacea** — slight reduction in cold duration in some trials **[Some/mixed]**.
- **Mullein (*Verbascum thapsus*)** — leaf tea for cough; **strain through fine cloth** to remove irritating hairs. **[Traditional]**
- **Pine needle tea** — vitamin C source (prevents scurvy). Avoid **yew** (deadly) and ponderosa/Norfolk Island pine; avoid in pregnancy. **[Good for vitamin C]**
- **Rose hips** — high vitamin C. **[Good for vitamin C]**
- **Garlic and wild onion** — mild antimicrobial in lab studies **[Some]**.
- **Ginger** (stored root) — nausea, motion sickness, morning sickness **[Good]**.

## Digestive

- **Peppermint / wild mint** — indigestion, IBS symptoms **[Good for IBS]**.
- **Blackberry root/leaf tea** — tannins traditionally used for diarrhea **[Traditional]**. Still give ORS.
- **Chamomile** — mild calming, digestive **[Some]**; ragweed allergy cross-reacts.
- **Activated charcoal** — not an herb, but useful for some ingestions (see *Low-Resource Chemistry*).

## Sleep and anxiety

- **Valerian root** — sleep **[Some/mixed]**.
- **Passionflower (maypop, Southeast)** — mild anxiety **[Some]**.
- **Lemon balm** — calming **[Some]**.

## Antimicrobial / antifungal (topical)

- **Usnea lichen** ("old man's beard", stringy lichen with a white elastic inner cord) — traditional wound dressing; lab antibacterial activity **[Traditional/lab]**.
- **Oregon grape / goldenseal (berberine)** — topical and traditional GI use; avoid in pregnancy and infants **[Some]**.
- **Tea tree oil** (stored) — fungal skin infections **[Some]**; toxic if swallowed.

## Herbs with important dangers

- **Sassafras root** — safrole is carcinogenic; occasional traditional tea only.
- **Labrador tea** (Alaska, Northern) — can be toxic in strong or frequent doses.
- **Chaparral/creosote bush** (Southwest) — internal use linked to liver failure.
- **Pennyroyal** — oil is deadly; tea dangerous in pregnancy.
- **Arnica** — topical only on unbroken skin; toxic internally.
- **Coltsfoot, borage, comfrey** — liver-toxic alkaloids internally.
