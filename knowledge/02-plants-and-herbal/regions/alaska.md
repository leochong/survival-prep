# Alaska: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**Zones:** Southeast coastal rainforest, Southcentral, Interior boreal forest (spruce/birch), Western/Arctic tundra. Very short intense growing season with near-24-hour summer daylight; long, extremely cold winters (−40° possible in Interior). Calories are scarce; plant foods are a supplement to fish and game.

## Top edible plants

### Berries (the main plant calorie source)
- **Blueberries/bog blueberries (*Vaccinium*), lingonberry/lowbush cranberry (*V. vitis-idaea*), highbush cranberry (*Viburnum edule*), cloudberry/salmonberry in Western AK (*Rubus chamaemorus* — single amber berry on a low plant), crowberry/blackberry (*Empetrum* — abundant, watery, black), nagoonberry, raspberry, salmonberry (coastal), currants, bearberry, serviceberry.**
- Lingonberries and crowberries persist under snow into winter and spring.
- No common Alaskan berry lookalikes are deadly **except baneberry** (*Actaea rubra* — red or white berries clustered on a single stalk above a leafy herb, not a shrub) and **some honeysuckle/twinberry** (avoid twinberry — paired black berries above red bracts).

### Fireweed (*Chamerion angustifolium*)
- Tall magenta flower spikes in burns and clearings. Young shoots (spring) and young leaves cooked or raw; flowers raw; pith of older stalks. Fermented leaves make tea (traditional "ivan chai").

### Spruce (*Picea*)
- Bright green spring tips: raw or tea, high vitamin C. Inner bark emergency food. Pitch as wound salve and fire starter.

### Rose hips (*Rosa acicularis*)
- Persist on bushes through winter; very high vitamin C. Remove hairy seeds (irritating to the gut).

### Other edibles
- **Wild chives and onions** (smell test), **sourdock** (*Rumex arcticus* — traditional fermented green in Western AK), **mountain sorrel** (*Oxyria*), **beach greens/sea purslane** (*Honckenya*), **goose tongue** (*Plantago maritima* — but avoid confusion with toxic arrowgrass), **wild celery/cow parsnip** (*Heracleum* — peeled stalks; phototoxic sap; experts only due to hemlock confusion), **dandelion, chickweed, lamb's quarters, nettle, fiddleheads (ostrich fern — cook), Eskimo potato (*Hedysarum alpinum* — roots; avoid confusion with *H. mackenzii*, wild sweet pea, which may be toxic)**, **bistort roots and bulblets**, **Labrador tea** (small amounts only).
- **Seaweeds:** coastal; similar rules as PNW. **Shellfish:** PSP is a major risk in Alaska — fatal cases occur from untested beaches.

## Medicinal plants
Yarrow, plantain, spruce pitch salve, devil's club (Southeast/Southcentral), stinkweed/wormwood (*Artemisia tilesii* — traditional wash, poultice, and tea in Western AK), willow bark, Labrador tea (caution), cottonwood bud salve, usnea, chaga (birch fungus — traditional tea; high oxalates, avoid with kidney disease), rose hips, coltsfoot (external).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Water hemlock (*Cicuta mackenzieana/douglasii*) — the most dangerous; monkshood; larkspur; baneberry; false hellebore; death camas (mountain death camas, *Anticlea elegans*); lupine; arrowgrass; wild sweet pea; poison water parsnip confusion; nightshade only in gardens. Animal: **polar bear and bearded seal livers** (hypervitaminosis A).

## Cold-weather nutrition note
In extreme cold, a person may need 4,000–6,000 kcal/day. Fat is essential — lean meat alone (rabbit starvation) causes illness. Combine plant foods with fish, fat, and game.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Spring (May–June):** fireweed shoots, spruce tips, fiddleheads, sourdock, nettle, overwintered lingonberries, cottonwood buds, birch sap.
- **Summer (June–Aug):** greens, salmonberries, cloudberries, early blueberries.
- **Late summer–fall (Aug–Sept):** blueberries, lingonberries, crowberries, highbush cranberries, rose hips, Eskimo potato roots.
- **Winter:** frozen berries on bushes, rose hips, spruce/pine tea, stored foods.
