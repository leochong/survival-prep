# California: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**Areas:** coast ranges, Central Valley, Sierra Nevada foothills, Southern California chaparral and coastal sage scrub. (Desert regions: see *Southwest*; far north coast: see *Pacific Northwest*.) Mediterranean climate: wet mild winters, long dry summers; fire is a major hazard.

## Top edible plants

### Acorns (oaks: valley, black, coast live, tanoak, blue)
- The traditional staple of California nations. Shell, grind, and **leach** (cold water through a sand basin or cloth bag for hours to days) until no bitterness remains; cook as mush or bread. Black oak and tanoak acorns were favored. Store whole acorns dry in a well-ventilated place.

### Miner's lettuce (*Claytonia perfoliata*)
- Winter–spring, shaded areas; round leaf with the flower stem passing through the center. Raw salad green.

### Manzanita (*Arctostaphylos*)
- Red-barked shrubs; small apple-like berries (summer) — make cider by crushing and soaking in cool water; dried and ground to meal.

### Toyon / California holly (*Heteromeles arbutifolia*)
- Red winter berries; **cook or dry them** (raw contain cyanogenic compounds). Bitter-sweet.

### California bay laurel (*Umbellularia californica*)
- Nuts (the "bay nut") **roasted** until dark taste like bitter chocolate/coffee. Leaves are much stronger than culinary bay — use sparingly.

### Chia (*Salvia columbariae*) and other sages
- Seeds high in fat and protein; soak or roast. White sage, black sage leaves as tea/flavor.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Blue elderberry (*Sambucus cerulea*):** cook berries; flowers for tea.
- **California blackberry, thimbleberry, wild strawberry, wild grape, gooseberry/currant, salal (north), lemonade berry (*Rhus integrifolia* — sour coating soaked for a drink), sugar bush.**
- **Cattail, tule (bulrush) roots and pollen, watercress (cook if water may be contaminated — liver fluke risk), wild radish, mustard greens, nasturtium (invasive), wild fennel (anise smell — but carrot family; confirm vs. poison hemlock), cheeseweed/mallow, chickweed, dock, purslane, lamb's quarters.**
- **Soaproot (*Chlorogalum*):** young shoots cooked; bulbs pit-roasted for long periods (raw contains saponins used as soap and fish poison) — experts only.
- **Pine nuts:** gray/foothill pine, sugar pine, pinyon on the east side.
- **Prickly pear** (south), **yucca/chaparral yucca flower stalks** (cooked).
- **Black walnut (California)**, **hazelnut** (north).
- **Coastal:** seaweeds (see PNW), mussels only with no red-tide risk (CA historically has an annual mussel quarantine May–October).

## Medicinal plants
Yerba santa (*Eriodictyon* — respiratory tea, traditional), yerba buena (mint tea), California mugwort (*Artemisia douglasiana* — traditional wash/poison oak remedy), white sage, black sage, yarrow, plantain, elderberry, willow, California poppy (mild sedative; [Some]), coyote brush, manzanita leaf (urinary, like uva-ursi; short-term).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Poison oak (everywhere below ~5,000 ft — the most common injury plant in CA), poison hemlock, water hemlock, oleander, castor bean, jimsonweed/sacred datura, tree tobacco, foxglove, death camas, larkspur, monkshood (Sierra), California buckeye (nuts toxic raw; traditionally leached), toyon raw berries, cherry/plum pits, pacific yew. Mushrooms: death cap, western destroying angel.

## Hazards
Wildfire season (summer–fall): know two evacuation routes; fire travels fast uphill and with wind. Rattlesnakes in foothills. Mountain lions. Earthquakes (see *Shelter* and *Disaster Response*).

## Seasonal calendar
- **Winter (rainy season):** miner's lettuce, chickweed, mustard, mallow, toyon (cooked), watercress, nettles, mushrooms (experts).
- **Spring:** wildflowers, yucca stalks, soaproot shoots, chia (late), elder flowers.
- **Summer:** blackberries, elderberries, manzanita berries, lemonade berry, pine nuts (late), prickly pear (south).
- **Fall:** acorns, bay nuts, black walnuts, grapes, pine nuts, rose hips.
