# Great Plains US: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States/areas:** North and South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, west and north Texas, eastern Montana, Wyoming and Colorado. Semi-arid to subhumid grassland; few trees except along rivers; harsh winters in the north, heat in the south; drought and wind are constant threats.

## Top edible plants

### Prairie turnip / timpsila (*Pediomelum esculentum*)
- **ID:** low hairy plant with palmate leaves (5 leaflets), blue-purple flower spikes in late spring; starchy tuber under the soil.
- **Use:** dig in early summer while tops are visible (they break off and blow away later). Peel and eat raw, roasted, or dried and ground to flour. A major traditional staple of Plains nations.

### Chokecherry (*Prunus virginiana*)
- **ID:** shrub/small tree, finely toothed oval leaves, drooping clusters of dark red/purple cherries (late summer).
- **Use:** fruit flesh in jelly, syrup, dried in pemmican. Traditionally pounded whole and dried — heat/drying breaks down some cyanogenic compounds, but **do not eat raw pits**.

### Wild plum (*Prunus americana*) and sand plum (*P. angustifolia*)
- Thickets along draws; red-yellow fruit in late summer. Eat fruit, discard pits.

### Buffaloberry (*Shepherdia argentea*)
- Thorny silvery-leaved shrub; small red berries (sweeter after frost). Contains saponins — eat in moderation.

### Sunflower (*Helianthus annuus*) and Jerusalem artichoke (*H. tuberosus*)
- Seeds of wild sunflowers (smaller than domestic); Jerusalem artichoke tubers in fall.

### Yucca / soapweed (*Yucca glauca*)
- Flowers (petals) and young green seed pods cooked; roots are soap (saponins) — not food.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Prickly pear (*Opuntia*):** pads (nopales) and fruit (tunas) — remove glochids by burning or peeling.
- **Groundplum milkvetch (*Astragalus crassicarpus*):** fleshy pods, raw or cooked — but **many other *Astragalus* (locoweeds) are toxic**; experts only.
- **Wild onion (*Allium*):** must smell of onion (death camas!).
- **Lamb's quarters, amaranth, purslane, dandelion, curly dock, wild lettuce (young), cattail (along water), bulrush roots and pollen.**
- **Hackberry (*Celtis*):** thin sweet flesh over a hard seed; whole berries can be crushed and eaten — a calorie-dense trail food.
- **Wild grape, mulberry, gooseberry, currant, serviceberry, wild strawberry, wild rose hips** along rivers.
- **Bur oak acorns** (leach), **black walnut and pecan** in eastern/southern edges.
- **Indian breadroot, ground bean, hog peanut** along woodland edges.

## Medicinal plants
Purple coneflower (*Echinacea angustifolia* — the classic Plains medicine), yarrow, plantain, prairie sage/fringed sage (*Artemisia* — smudge, wound wash, traditional), wild bergamot, horsemint, prairie licorice root, curlycup gumweed (*Grindelia* — traditional for poison ivy and cough), wild rose, willow along rivers, cedar/juniper.

## Regional toxic plants of note
Death camas, locoweeds (*Oxytropis*, *Astragalus* — neurotoxic, mainly to livestock but human risk too), water hemlock (along water), poison hemlock, jimsonweed and sacred datura, larkspur, lupine (many species toxic), nightshades, milkweeds (raw), chokecherry pits/leaves. Rattlesnakes are common.

## Water note
Water is the limiting factor on the Plains. Cottonwoods and willows indicate shallow groundwater; cattails and bulrushes indicate standing water. See *Finding Water*.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Spring:** wild onion, dandelion, lamb's quarters, cattail shoots, yucca flowers (late).
- **Early summer:** prairie turnip (dig), groundplum pods, mulberries, serviceberries.
- **Late summer:** chokecherry, plum, buffaloberry (after frost best), sunflower seeds, prickly pear fruit.
- **Fall/Winter:** Jerusalem artichoke, acorns, hackberries (persist into winter), rose hips, cattail starch.
