# Hawaii: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**Islands:** tropical climate with windward (wet) and leeward (dry) sides; high-elevation zones on Maui and the Big Island can freeze. Most food plants are Polynesian introductions ("canoe plants") or later introductions; native ecosystems are fragile — avoid harvesting rare native species.

**Island reality:** Hawaii imports most of its food. In a long supply disruption, traditional agriculture (lo'i kalo, breadfruit groves, fishponds) is far more important than foraging.

## Top edible plants

### Kalo / taro (*Colocasia esculenta*)
- Large heart-shaped leaves with the stem attached in the middle of the leaf (peltate).
- Corms and leaves (lū'au) must be **cooked thoroughly** — boil corms 1+ hour; leaves at least 45 minutes or until soft — to destroy calcium oxalate. Pound cooked corm into poi (ferments and stores well).
- Related aroids (ape, *Alocasia*) are more irritating; some ornamentals (dieffenbachia, *Xanthosoma* raw) are toxic.

### 'Ulu / breadfruit (*Artocarpus altilis*)
- Large tree with deeply lobed leaves; green football-sized fruit. Roast, boil, or steam mature fruit; ferment for storage. Extremely productive (100s of fruit per tree).

### Niu / coconut (*Cocos nucifera*)
- Green coconuts for water (sterile inside, good hydration), mature for meat and oil. Coconut "heart" and sprouted coconut (coconut "apple") are edible. Watch for falling nuts.

### Kī / ti (*Cordyline fruticosa*)
- Root (huge, sugary) baked for long periods; leaves wrap food, thatch, and make rain capes and plates. Leaves are not food.

### Other edibles
- **Mai'a / banana** (and wild bananas), **'uala / sweet potato** (leaves too, cooked), **uhi / yam** (cook thoroughly; some wild yams toxic), **pia / Polynesian arrowroot** (requires processing), **kukui nut** (candlenut — **toxic raw**, strongly purgative; must be roasted and eaten in very small amounts; mostly used for oil/light).
- **Feral fruit:** guava and strawberry guava, mango (sap of skin/tree causes poison-ivy-like rash — related to poison ivy), mountain apple ('ōhi'a 'ai), passion fruit (lilikoi), papaya, avocado, Java plum, Surinam cherry, thimbleberry ('ākala — native Hawaiian raspberry), 'ōhelo (native blueberry relative near volcanoes — traditionally an offering to Pele first).
- **Greens:** purslane ('ihi), pōpolo (*Solanum americanum* — ripe black berries and well-cooked leaves are traditional; **unripe green berries toxic**), sweet potato leaves, chayote, watercress (lowland streams — leptospirosis risk; cook), hō'i'o fern (*Diplazium esculentum* — common edible fern shoot).
- **Limu (seaweed):** limu kohu, limu 'ele'ele, ogo (*Gracilaria*), wawae'iole — traditional and nutritious. Avoid limu near sewage/runoff. Learn from local knowledge; a few toxic marine cyanobacteria exist.

## Medicinal plants (lā'au lapa'au — traditional)
Noni (*Morinda citrifolia* — fruit poultice for wounds/bruises; juice has caused liver injury in rare reports), 'ōlena (turmeric), 'awa (kava — calming; liver-toxicity reports with heavy use), ki leaves (cooling wrap for fever, headache), kukui (sap for mouth sores, oil for skin), 'uhaloa (traditional sore throat), aloe, lemongrass, ginger, guava leaf tea (diarrhea, traditional).

## Regional toxic plants and hazards
Be-still/yellow oleander, oleander, angel's trumpet (*Brugmansia*) and datura, rosary pea (seeds deadly), castor bean, crown flower and milkweeds (sap), pencil tree/firestick (*Euphorbia* sap blinds), dieffenbachia, raw kukui, raw taro, unripe pōpolo, cassava (cook properly — cyanide). **Leptospirosis** in streams and muddy water (rat urine) — never drink untreated stream water; cover cuts. **Ciguatera** fish poisoning from large reef fish. Box jellyfish and Portuguese man o' war (see *Venomous Bites and Stings*). Centipedes.

## Seasonal notes
Year-round growing; breadfruit peaks in summer–fall with a smaller winter crop; mango spring–summer; guava most of the year; 'ōhelo at high elevation summer–fall.
