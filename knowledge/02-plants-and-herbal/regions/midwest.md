# Midwest US: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States:** Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri. Continental climate, cold winters, hot humid summers; mix of hardwood forest, oak savanna, prairie remnants, and farmland.

## Top edible plants

### Wild rice (*Zizania palustris/aquatica*)
- Shallow lakes and slow rivers, especially Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan. Harvested late August–September by bending stalks over a canoe and tapping. Parch (dry-roast) and thresh. High calorie and protein. Rights to harvest in some areas belong to tribal nations — respect them.
- **Ergot warning:** discard grains with purplish-black, elongated growths (ergot fungus — toxic).

### Mulberry (*Morus*)
- Trees with variable (mitten-shaped or simple) leaves; blackberry-like fruit, June–July. Unripe fruit and leaves can cause stomach upset; eat ripe fruit.

### Black walnut (*Juglans nigra*) and hickory (*Carya*)
- Fall nuts; very high fat and calories. Walnut husks stain heavily (and are a dye). Hickory nuts can be boiled and the oil skimmed ("hickory milk").

### Elderberry (*Sambucus canadensis*)
- Shrub with opposite compound leaves, flat-topped white flower clusters (June), purple-black berries (Aug–Sept).
- Cook berries; strain seeds. Flowers can be battered and fried or made into tea.
- **Lookalikes:** water hemlock has umbrella-shaped flower clusters on a non-woody herb; pokeweed berries hang in long drooping chains with red stems. Elder is a woody shrub.

### Stinging nettle (*Urtica dioica*)
- Opposite toothed leaves with stinging hairs, square stem, moist ground. Spring tops boiled or steamed (stings vanish with cooking or drying). Very nutritious; tea as well.

### Morels (*Morchella*)
- April–May near dying elms, old apple orchards, ash, and cottonwoods. Must be **completely hollow**; see *Mushroom Safety* for false morels.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Cattail, acorns, hazelnut, butternut, wild plum, chokecherry (pits toxic), serviceberry/juneberry, blackberry, black raspberry, wild strawberry, gooseberry/currant, wild grape, pawpaw (southern Midwest), American persimmon (southern Midwest).**
- **Jerusalem artichoke (*Helianthus tuberosus*):** tall sunflower with tubers; dig after frost. Causes gas — cook long.
- **Lamb's quarters, amaranth, purslane, dandelion, chickweed, wood sorrel, curly dock, garlic mustard, wild onion/leek.**
- **Milkweed (*Asclepias syriaca*)**: young shoots, flower buds, immature pods — cook in boiling water. **Lookalike:** dogbane (toxic) has thinner, branched red stems, smaller leaves, no hairs on underside. Experts only.
- **Sunchokes, groundnut, Solomon's seal shoots (true Solomon's seal; false Solomon's seal is also edible — but young shoots resemble toxic false hellebore)**.
- **Sumac lemonade (staghorn/smooth sumac).**

## Medicinal plants
Plantain, yarrow, nettle, elderberry, echinacea (purple coneflower — native prairie plant), wild bergamot (bee balm), mullein, willow, boneset, slippery elm, jewelweed, prickly ash (traditional "toothache tree"), wild cherry bark (traditional cough syrup — contains cyanogenic compounds; dried bark only, short use).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Water hemlock, poison hemlock (abundant along roadsides), wild parsnip (phototoxic), white snakeroot (causes milk sickness), pokeweed, jimsonweed, moonseed, Kentucky coffee tree seeds (raw), buckeye (Ohio buckeye), nightshades, May apple (unripe), poison ivy, dogbane, baneberry, lily of the valley.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Spring:** nettle, morels, ramps (wooded areas), dandelion, garlic mustard, milkweed shoots, cattail shoots.
- **Summer:** mulberries, black raspberries, serviceberries, elder flowers, milkweed buds/pods, purslane.
- **Late summer/fall:** wild rice, elderberries, plums, grapes, black walnut, hickory, hazelnut, acorns, Jerusalem artichoke, pawpaw, persimmons.
- **Winter:** stored nuts and rice, cattail rhizomes, rose hips, inner bark (emergency), pine/spruce tea.
