# Northeast US: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States:** Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, northern West Virginia. Humid continental climate; mixed hardwood/conifer forest; growing season 90–180 days.

## Top edible plants

### Cattail (*Typha*)
- **ID:** wetlands; tall flat strap leaves; brown "hot dog" seed head.
- **Use:** spring shoots (peel, eat raw or cooked like cucumber); green flower spikes boiled like corn; yellow pollen as flour; rhizome starch year-round (crush in water, let starch settle).
- **Lookalike:** young iris (blue flag) is toxic — iris leaves are flat in a fan with no cattail stalk and no mucilage; cattail leaf bases are slimy-mucilaginous and wrapped around each other.
- Avoid polluted water.

### Ramps / wild leeks (*Allium tricoccum*)
- **ID:** rich moist woods in early spring; 1–3 broad smooth leaves, red/purple stem base, strong onion-garlic smell.
- **Use:** leaves and bulbs, raw or cooked. Harvest one leaf per plant to sustain patches.
- **Lookalikes (deadly):** lily of the valley, false hellebore. **Crush and smell** — no onion smell = do not eat.

### Fiddleheads — ostrich fern (*Matteuccia struthiopteris*)
- **ID:** smooth, deep U-shaped groove on the inside of the stem, brown papery scales; no fuzz.
- **Use:** boil 15 minutes (or steam 10–12) — undercooked fiddleheads cause GI illness.
- **Avoid:** bracken fern (triangular frond; carcinogenic) and other ferns.

### Sugar maple and other maples (*Acer*)
- Late winter sap (freezing nights, thawing days) is drinkable fresh and boils down ~40:1 to syrup. Inner bark edible in emergencies. Box elder and birch can also be tapped.

### Acorns (oak, *Quercus*)
- Abundant fall calories (fat and carbohydrate). Shell, crush, and **leach tannins**: cold-water leaching (change water several times daily for days until no longer bitter) or boil in changes of water. Dry and grind to flour. White oak group needs less leaching than red oak group.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Dandelion:** leaves (spring, before flowering), flowers, roots (roasted as coffee substitute).
- **Wild blueberry, blackberry, raspberry, black cap, wild strawberry, huckleberry:** summer fruit; no deadly aggregate-berry lookalikes in the region.
- **Staghorn sumac:** fuzzy red upright berry clusters (Aug–Sept) make a vitamin-C "lemonade" (soak cold, strain through cloth). **Poison sumac** has hanging white/grey berries and grows in swamps.
- **Black walnut, hickory, beechnut, hazelnut:** fall nuts; high calorie.
- **Wild grape:** see moonseed warning in *Deadly Plants*.
- **Garlic mustard (invasive):** leaves and roots, raw or cooked; abundant spring green.
- **Japanese knotweed (invasive):** young shoots taste like rhubarb.
- **Chickweed, purslane, lamb's quarters, wood sorrel, violets:** salad greens (sorrel in moderation — oxalates).
- **Wintergreen / teaberry:** leaves for tea, red berries edible.
- **Groundnut (*Apios americana*):** vine with tuber strings along moist banks; cook tubers. Excellent calorie source.

## Medicinal plants
Plantain, yarrow, jewelweed (poison ivy), willow bark, elderberry (cook), mullein, white pine needle tea (vitamin C; avoid yew), usnea, goldenrod tea (traditional for urinary complaints), bee balm (traditional throat/cough tea), boneset (traditional fever tea; toxic in large amounts).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Water hemlock, poison hemlock, false hellebore, lily of the valley, white baneberry, pokeweed, jimsonweed, moonseed, mountain laurel, rhododendron (honey from them can be toxic), poison ivy, poison sumac (swamps), wild parsnip (phototoxic), giant hogweed (NY, PA, New England — severe burns), yew in landscaping. Death cap and destroying angel mushrooms are present.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Late winter:** maple sap, inner bark, cattail rhizome starch, rose hips, white pine tea.
- **Spring:** ramps, fiddleheads, dandelion, garlic mustard, knotweed, nettle, cattail shoots, violets.
- **Summer:** berries, cattail flower/pollen, purslane, lamb's quarters, groundnut flowers.
- **Fall:** acorns, walnuts, hickory, beech, grapes, sumac, groundnut tubers, Jerusalem artichoke.
