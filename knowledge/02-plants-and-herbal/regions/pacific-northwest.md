# Pacific Northwest: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States/areas:** western Washington and Oregon (west of the Cascades), coastal northern California. Mild, wet winters, dry summers; temperate rainforest of Douglas-fir, western redcedar, western hemlock, Sitka spruce.

## Top edible plants

### Salmonberry (*Rubus spectabilis*) and thimbleberry (*R. parviflorus*)
- Salmonberry: thorny canes, magenta flowers, yellow-to-red raspberry-like fruit in May–June; young peeled shoots edible.
- Thimbleberry: no thorns, huge soft maple-like leaves, flat red berries in summer. (Leaves make emergency toilet paper.)

### Salal (*Gaultheria shallon*)
- Dense evergreen shrub with leathery leaves; dark blue-black berries July–September. Very abundant; dried into cakes traditionally.

### Evergreen and red huckleberry, blackberry (Himalayan, invasive), trailing blackberry, Oregon grape (tart), elderberry (blue elder — cook; red elderberry is more toxic, avoid unless traditionally processed), wild strawberry, Pacific crabapple, serviceberry.

### Stinging nettle (*Urtica dioica*)
- Abundant spring green; cook or dry. Also fiber for cordage.

### Wapato / arrowhead (*Sagittaria latifolia*)
- Arrow-shaped leaves in shallow water; tubers released by treading mud in fall; roast or boil.

### Camas (*Camassia*)
- Western Oregon/Washington prairies (e.g., Willamette Valley); see death camas warning in *Rocky Mountains* guide. Pit-roast bulbs.

### Ferns
- **Licorice fern rhizome** (on mossy maple trunks): sweet, chewed for sore throat.
- **Sword fern and bracken:** bracken fiddleheads are carcinogenic — avoid.

### Coastal and marine foods
- **Seaweeds:** nearly all PNW seaweeds are non-toxic (exception: acid kelp, *Desmarestia* — sour taste, causes GI distress). Bull kelp, nori (*Pyropia*), sea lettuce, kombu. Harvest attached living seaweed from clean water; dry for storage. Rich in minerals and iodine.
- **Shellfish:** mussels, clams, oysters — **only if no paralytic shellfish poisoning (PSP) risk**. PSP toxin is not destroyed by cooking and can kill within hours (numb lips, tingling, paralysis). In a grid-down scenario with no testing, risk is highest in summer; be extremely cautious.

### Other reliable edibles
Cattail, miner's lettuce (*Claytonia perfoliata* — round leaf pierced by the stem), chickweed, dandelion, sheep sorrel, wood sorrel, fireweed, bigleaf maple (sap and flowers), hazelnut (beaked hazelnut), Garry oak acorns (leach), spruce tips (spring vitamin C), cat's ear, dock, plantain.

## Medicinal plants
- **Devil's club (*Oplopanax horridus*):** spiny shrub; inner root bark used traditionally for many ailments (respiratory, diabetes). Spines cause painful infected punctures. **[Traditional]**
- **Oregon grape root:** berberine, topical antimicrobial and traditional GI bitter.
- **Western redcedar:** traditional antimicrobial and woven into everything; leaf tea in small amounts only (thujone).
- **Yarrow, plantain, nettle, usnea, cottonwood buds (balm of Gilead — resinous salve for sore muscles and wounds), Douglas-fir pitch, willow bark, Labrador tea (bog; caution), self-heal, horsetail (silica; do not use long-term).**

## Regional toxic plants of note
Water hemlock (*Cicuta douglasii*), poison hemlock, Pacific yew, foxglove (abundant along roads and clearings — leaves resemble comfrey/mullein in rosettes), false hellebore/skunk cabbage confusion (skunk cabbage raw is acrid with calcium oxalate), baneberry, death camas, red elderberry (raw), English ivy, Scotch broom seeds, tansy ragwort, poison oak (Oregon west of the Cascades, south). Death cap mushrooms established in Oregon and Washington cities.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Late winter–spring:** nettle, miner's lettuce, salmonberry shoots, spruce tips, licorice fern, bigleaf maple flowers, seaweed (spring low tides).
- **Early summer:** salmonberries, thimbleberries, wild strawberries, camas.
- **Late summer:** salal, huckleberries, blackberries, Oregon grape, serviceberries.
- **Fall:** hazelnuts, acorns, wapato tubers, crabapples, rose hips.
