# Rocky Mountains and Intermountain West: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States/areas:** Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado, Utah mountains, northern New Mexico highlands, eastern Oregon/Washington ranges. Elevation drives everything: sagebrush steppe → ponderosa/pinyon-juniper → spruce-fir → alpine tundra. Short growing seasons (often under 100 days at elevation); snow possible any month above 9,000 ft.

## Top edible plants

### Huckleberry (*Vaccinium*)
- Mid–high elevation, often in old burns; small blue-purple berries, July–September. A top calorie source — and a top bear food. Make noise.

### Serviceberry / saskatoon (*Amelanchier alnifolia*)
- Shrub with oval leaves toothed only at the top half; white spring flowers; purple berries in July. Abundant; dried into cakes traditionally. Seeds contain small amounts of cyanogenic compounds — fine in normal quantities.

### Blue camas (*Camassia quamash*)
- Wet spring meadows; brilliant blue flowers. Bulbs must be **slow-cooked (pit-roasted 24+ hours)** to convert inulin to digestible sugars.
- **Death camas** grows in the same meadows with cream-white flowers. Dig only while blue camas is in flower, and mark plants.

### Biscuitroot / lomatium (*Lomatium* spp.)
- Traditional root staple, dried and ground. **Carrot family** — requires expert ID; confusion with poison/water hemlock is fatal.

### Bitterroot (*Lewisia rediviva*)
- Montana state flower; roots peeled and boiled in spring before flowering. Low calories but traditional.

### Pine nuts and pine products
- **Limber pine, whitebark pine (protected/declining), pinyon**: nuts in fall.
- **Inner bark (cambium)** of lodgepole and ponderosa: emergency food in spring, scraped in strips. Kills or damages the tree.
- **Needle tea** for vitamin C; avoid ponderosa needles for pregnant people (abortifacient in cattle).

### Other reliable edibles
- **Glacier lily (*Erythronium grandiflorum*):** yellow nodding flowers near snowmelt; corm and leaves edible cooked (eat leaves sparingly; mildly emetic in quantity).
- **Spring beauty (*Claytonia*):** small corms ("mountain potato") and leaves.
- **Wild strawberry, raspberry, thimbleberry, gooseberry, currant, chokecherry (flesh only), Oregon grape (tart berries), rose hips, kinnikinnick (bearberry — bland, mealy).**
- **Fireweed:** young shoots and leaves; pith of older stalks.
- **Cattail, bulrush, arrowhead/wapato** in valley wetlands.
- **Wild onion** (smell test), **yampa** (*Perideridia* — carrot family, experts only).
- **Dandelion, lamb's quarters, mountain sorrel, miner's lettuce, stinging nettle, sheep sorrel.**
- **Sego lily (*Calochortus*):** Utah state flower; bulbs eaten by Mormon pioneers in famine — but resembles death camas when not flowering.

## Medicinal plants
- **Arnica (*Arnica cordifolia*):** external only, on unbroken skin for bruises/sprains.
- **Osha (*Ligusticum porteri*):** root traditionally for respiratory infections — **carrot-family hemlock confusion risk**; osha root has a strong celery-like/spicy smell and hairy "collar" of old leaf stems.
- **Yarrow, plantain, nettle, Oregon grape root (berberine), usnea, pine/spruce resin, wild rose, willow bark, uva-ursi/kinnikinnick leaf** (traditional urinary antiseptic; short-term only), **sagebrush** (*Artemisia tridentata* — traditional wash and smudge; strong, not for internal use in quantity), **mullein**.

## Regional toxic plants of note
Death camas, water hemlock, poison hemlock, larkspur (*Delphinium*), monkshood (*Aconitum* — deadly, blue hooded flowers in moist meadows), lupine, false hellebore/corn lily (*Veratrum californicum*), baneberry, locoweed, western yew (Pacific yew in northern Idaho/Montana).

## Mountain hazards
Hypothermia even in summer, lightning above treeline (descend before afternoon storms), altitude illness above 8,000 ft (headache, nausea — descend if worsening; confusion or breathlessness at rest is an emergency), grizzly and black bears (store food 100 yd from camp, hang or use canisters, carry bear spray).

## Seasonal calendar
- **Spring (snowmelt, May–June):** glacier lily, spring beauty, bitterroot, nettle, dandelion, cattail shoots, inner bark.
- **Early summer:** camas (in bloom), biscuitroot, fireweed shoots, wild onion.
- **Mid–late summer:** serviceberries, huckleberries, raspberries, thimbleberries, currants, chokecherries.
- **Fall:** pine nuts, rose hips, Oregon grape, kinnikinnick, chokecherries (late).
