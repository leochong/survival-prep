# Southeast US: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States:** Virginia, southern West Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee, North and South Carolina, Georgia, Florida, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, Arkansas. Humid subtropical; long growing season (200–300+ days); south Florida is tropical.

## Top edible plants

### Muscadine and wild grapes (*Vitis*)
- **ID:** woody vines with forked tendrils; muscadines bear thick-skinned bronze/purple fruit (Aug–Oct).
- **Use:** fruit raw, juice, dried; young leaves can wrap food.
- **Lookalike:** moonseed (single crescent seed, no tendrils) — toxic. Porcelain berry (invasive) — not edible.

### American persimmon (*Diospyros virginiana*)
- **ID:** tree with blocky "alligator skin" bark; orange fruit 1–2 inches.
- **Use:** eat only when fully ripe, soft and wrinkled (often after first frost) — unripe fruit is intensely astringent and large amounts of unripe fruit can form stomach bezoars. Leaf tea is a vitamin C source.

### Pawpaw (*Asimina triloba*)
- **ID:** understory tree in river bottoms, large drooping leaves, green mango-like fruit in late summer.
- **Use:** custard-like ripe fruit; do not eat seeds or skin (some people get GI upset).

### Kudzu (*Pueraria montana*, invasive)
- Young leaves, shoots, and flowers cooked; starchy root (dig and extract starch). Abundant. Leaves are hairy — cook them. Do not confuse with poison ivy (kudzu leaves are larger, hairy, and the vine is hairy too).

### Saw palmetto and cabbage palm (*Sabal palmetto*)
- Cabbage palm heart (growing tip) is edible raw or cooked, but harvesting kills the palm — emergency only.
- Saw palmetto berries are edible but taste strongly; traditional prostate remedy.

### Maypop / purple passionflower (*Passiflora incarnata*)
- Vine with intricate purple flowers; egg-shaped fruit, edible pulp when wrinkled and ripe (late summer). Leaves are a traditional calming tea.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Pecan, hickory, black walnut, chinquapin, acorns (leached)** — fall nuts and calories.
- **Blackberry, dewberry, mulberry, blueberry, elderberry (cooked), wild plum, mayhaw, serviceberry.**
- **Cattail, dandelion, chickweed, henbit, wild onion (smell test!), purslane, lamb's quarters, amaranth, wood sorrel, clover.**
- **Prickly pear (*Opuntia humifusa*):** pads and fruit — burn or scrape off glochids (tiny hair-like spines).
- **Greenbrier (*Smilax*):** tender shoot tips raw or cooked; tuberous roots yield starch.
- **Sassafras:** leaves dried and powdered (filé) for thickening; see root caution in *Medicinal Herbs*.
- **Coconut, sea grape, Spanish bayonet (yucca) flowers, coontie (requires extensive processing — toxic raw; avoid)** in Florida.
- **Wild rice (*Zizania*) and water lotus (*Nelumbo*)**: lotus seeds and tubers edible cooked.

## Medicinal plants
Plantain, yarrow, rabbit tobacco (traditional respiratory), mullein, elderberry, passionflower, wild ginger (topical only — aristolochic acid; do not ingest), sweetgum resin (traditional wound salve), slippery elm inner bark (sore throat, GI), sumac, pine needle tea, beautyberry leaves (crushed leaves as a traditional insect repellent — studied by USDA).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Pokeweed, water hemlock, poison hemlock, jimsonweed, Carolina jessamine (yellow jessamine — all parts toxic), oleander, rosary pea (south FL — deadly seeds), castor bean, manchineel (south FL), buckeye, mountain laurel, poison ivy, poison oak, poison sumac, coontie raw, Brazilian pepper (irritant). Snakes: copperheads, cottonmouths, rattlesnakes, coral snakes — watch where you reach.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Winter:** persimmons (early), greens (chickweed, henbit, wild onion, dandelion), cattail starch, pine tea.
- **Spring:** greenbrier tips, kudzu shoots, dewberries (late), mulberries, elder flowers.
- **Summer:** blackberries, blueberries, plums, pawpaw (late), maypop (late), prickly pear.
- **Fall:** muscadines, persimmons, pecans, hickory, acorns, chinquapins.
