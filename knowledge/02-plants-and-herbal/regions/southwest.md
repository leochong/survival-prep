# Desert Southwest US: Wild Edibles and Medicinals

**States/areas:** Arizona, New Mexico, southern Nevada, southern Utah, west Texas, southeastern California deserts (Mojave, Sonoran, Chihuahuan). Arid; extreme heat in summer, cold desert nights, monsoon storms July–September; sky-island mountains hold pine/oak forests.

## Top edible plants

### Mesquite (*Prosopis*)
- **ID:** thorny shrub/tree, feathery compound leaves; long tan bean pods in summer.
- **Use:** dry, yellow-tan pods (picked from the tree, not the ground — ground pods may carry aflatoxin mold) ground into sweet flour. Very important traditional staple. Discard pods with black spots/mold.

### Prickly pear (*Opuntia*)
- **Pads (nopales):** young spring pads; scrape off spines and glochids, slice, cook.
- **Fruit (tunas):** ripe red/purple in late summer; burn or roll on gravel to remove glochids; eat pulp, strain seeds or swallow whole. Use tongs/gloves.

### Agave / century plant (*Agave*)
- Traditional staple: the heart is **pit-roasted for 1–3 days** (raw agave is caustic, with irritating sap and saponins). Flower stalk (before blooming) can be roasted.
- Raw sap causes dermatitis.

### Yucca (*Yucca baccata* — banana yucca)
- Fleshy fruits baked or roasted; flowers cooked. Roots are soap, not food.

### Cholla buds (*Cylindropuntia*)
- Spring flower buds, de-spined (brush in a screen or roll in gravel), boiled or steamed. High calcium.

### Saguaro fruit (*Carnegiea*)
- Red fruit atop the cactus in June; pulp and seeds eaten fresh, dried, or cooked into syrup. Use a long pole.

### Pinyon pine nuts (*Pinus edulis, P. monophylla*)
- Fall harvest from cones in foothills/mountains. High calorie and fat. Roast cones near the fire to open them.

### Other reliable edibles
- **Juniper berries (*Juniperus*):** small amounts as flavoring/tea; avoid in pregnancy and with kidney issues; some species are bitter/irritant.
- **Desert chia (*Salvia columbariae*):** seeds, soaked or roasted.
- **Palo verde seeds:** green seeds raw like peas; mature seeds cooked.
- **Ironwood seeds:** roast — raw seeds can be mildly toxic.
- **Wolfberry/desert thorn (*Lycium*):** small red berries.
- **Purslane, amaranth (pigweed), lamb's quarters, tansy mustard, wild onion** after rains.
- **Acorns of Emory oak** (sweet, little leaching), **walnuts** in canyons, **hackberry**.
- **Barrel cactus fruit (*Ferocactus wislizeni*):** yellow fruits are edible (seeds roasted). **Do not drink the inner pulp juice** as a water source — it contains oxalic acid and causes diarrhea and vomiting.

## Medicinal plants
- **Creosote bush (*Larrea*, chaparral):** traditional external wash for wounds and skin; **internal use can cause liver failure**.
- **Aloe** (garden), **desert lavender, brittlebush resin, jojoba oil** (skin), **yerba mansa** (*Anemopsis* — traditional antimicrobial wash, wetlands), **Mormon tea/joint fir** (*Ephedra* — stimulant; dangerous for the heart in larger doses), **osha** (mountains — lookalike poison hemlock!), **globemallow** (soothing mucilage), **prickly pear pads** (sliced fillet as burn/wound poultice, traditional).

## Regional toxic plants of note
Sacred datura and jimsonweed, desert tobacco, oleander (landscaping, everywhere in desert cities), castor bean, tree tobacco, poison hemlock and water hemlock near water, larkspur, lupine, locoweed, desert rose. **Animals:** rattlesnakes (many species), Arizona bark scorpion, Gila monster, black widows — see *Venomous Bites and Stings*.

## Desert water warnings
Cacti are not reliable water sources. Find water at canyon bottoms, outside bends of dry streambeds (dig), under cottonwoods/willows/salt cedar, at rock tanks (tinajas) in shaded rock depressions, and by following birds at dawn/dusk. Travel at night in summer. See *Finding Water*.

## Seasonal calendar
- **Spring (Mar–May):** cholla buds, prickly pear pads, agave stalks, wild greens after winter rains, chia, palo verde flowers.
- **Early summer (Jun):** saguaro fruit, mesquite pods (Jun–Aug), palo verde seeds.
- **Monsoon (Jul–Sep):** purslane, amaranth, prickly pear fruit, banana yucca fruit, wolfberry.
- **Fall:** pinyon nuts, acorns, juniper berries, walnuts.
