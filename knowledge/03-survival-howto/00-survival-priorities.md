# Survival Priorities and the Rule of Threes

## Rule of threes (rough guide)

You can survive about:
- **3 minutes** without air (or with severe bleeding)
- **3 hours** without shelter in harsh weather (hypothermia/heat stroke)
- **3 days** without water
- **3 weeks** without food

Priorities therefore run: **immediate safety and first aid → shelter/thermal regulation → water → fire → signaling/communication → food**. Adjust to conditions: in the desert, water and shade jump to the top; in a winter storm, shelter and fire.

## S.T.O.P. when things go wrong

- **S**top — don't run or panic. Sit down.
- **T**hink — what happened, what do I have, what's the threat?
- **O**bserve — surroundings, weather, resources, people, injuries.
- **P**lan — choose actions in priority order, then act.

## Stay or go?

**Shelter in place** when your current location has water, shelter, supplies, and is not in the direct threat path. Most disaster survivors do best at home with stored supplies.

**Evacuate** when the location is threatened (fire, flood, toxic release, structural damage, unrest, long-term no-water). Leave **early** — roads clog fast.

Decision factors: threat timeline, water availability, medical needs, group mobility (children, elderly), fuel, destination with known resources, route safety.

## Minimum household stock (start here)

- Water: 1 gallon (4 L) per person per day, at least 2 weeks.
- Food: 2+ weeks of non-perishable food you already eat; manual can opener.
- Light: flashlights/headlamps, batteries, candles/lanterns (fire safety).
- Heat/cooking: camp stove with fuel; never use grills/generators indoors (carbon monoxide).
- Medical: kit plus 30–90 days of prescriptions.
- Sanitation: buckets, heavy trash bags, sawdust/cat litter, bleach, soap.
- Communication: battery/hand-crank NOAA weather radio, two-way radios, paper contact list.
- Documents and cash in small bills.
- Tools: fixed-blade knife, multitool, saw, shovel, duct tape, cordage, tarps, work gloves.
- Printed copies of key references (this knowledge base can be printed).

## Psychology

Most survival deaths involve a failure of will or judgment, not lack of gear.
- Create routines and assign jobs; purpose fights despair.
- Sleep is a survival resource — rotate watches.
- Sanitation and hygiene preserve morale and health.
- Accept the situation quickly; denial wastes the first critical hours.
- Small wins (fire lit, water boiled) build momentum.
- In groups: clear leadership, shared plans, conflict resolution early, care for the vulnerable.

## Group roles (example)

Leader/coordinator; medic; water and sanitation; food and cooking; security/watch; communications; scouting/foraging; childcare. Cross-train everyone in bleeding control, water treatment, and fire.
