# Water Purification and Disinfection

Waterborne illness (giardia, cryptosporidium, cholera, E. coli, hepatitis A, typhoid) is a leading killer after disasters. Treat **all** water from untreated sources, including clear mountain streams.

## Step 1: Choose the best source

Best to worst: sealed stored water → rainwater (clean collection surface) → springs at the point of emergence → deep wells → fast-flowing streams → lakes/ponds → stagnant water. Avoid water near mines, farms, industrial sites, septic systems, or with oily sheen, odd smell, or dead animals.

Indoor emergency sources: water heater tank (turn off power/gas, drain from the bottom valve), toilet **tank** (not the bowl; not if it contains chemical cleaners), pipes (open the lowest faucet after opening a high one), ice cubes, canned food liquids.

## Step 2: Clarify

Cloudy water protects germs from disinfectants. Let it settle for hours, then pour off the clear top. Filter through cloth, a coffee filter, or a sand/charcoal filter (see *Building: Biosand and Charcoal Filters*).

## Step 3: Disinfect

### Boiling (most reliable)
- Bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**. Above 6,500 feet (2,000 m), **boil for 3 minutes**.
- Let cool covered. Improve flat taste by pouring between two clean containers.

### Household bleach (sodium hypochlorite)
Use regular unscented bleach. Check the label for concentration.

| Bleach strength | Per 1 gallon (≈4 L) clear water | Per 1 liter |
|---|---|---|
| 6% | 8 drops (≈ 1/8 teaspoon) | 2 drops |
| 8.25% | 6 drops (≈ 1/8 teaspoon) | 2 drops |

- **Double the dose** for cloudy, very cold, or colored water (after clarifying).
- Stir and **wait 30 minutes**. It should have a slight chlorine smell. If not, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.
- Bleach loses strength over time (roughly 20% per year after opening, faster in heat); old bleach needs more drops.
- Chlorine does not reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*. Boil or filter (1 micron or smaller) when that is a concern.

### Pool shock (calcium hypochlorite) — long-term storage
Granular calcium hypochlorite (about 65–78%, no algaecides/clarifiers) stores far longer than liquid bleach.
1. Make a stock solution: dissolve **1 heaping teaspoon (≈ 1/4 oz) in 2 gallons** of water.
2. Disinfect: add **1 part stock solution to 100 parts water** (1 pint stock per 12.5 gallons).
3. Wait 30 minutes.
Store the dry granules in a cool, dry, ventilated place away from other chemicals, fuel, and organics — it is a strong oxidizer and fire hazard.

### Iodine
- 2% tincture of iodine: 5 drops per liter (10 if cloudy), wait 30 minutes.
- Not for pregnant people, those with thyroid problems or iodine allergy, or long-term continuous use (more than a few weeks).

### Commercial options
Water filters rated to 0.1–0.2 microns remove bacteria and protozoa but **not viruses** (pair with chlorine for viral risk). Chlorine dioxide tablets kill all three groups including crypto (wait 4 hours for crypto).

### Solar disinfection (SODIS)
- Fill clear **PET plastic bottles** (2 liters or smaller) with clear water (low turbidity).
- Lay them on a reflective surface (metal roof) in full sun for **6 hours**; on cloudy days use **2 consecutive days**.
- Not effective for cloudy water or in glass/colored bottles. Kills most pathogens via UV and heat.

### Distillation
Boil water and collect the steam on a cool surface (lid tilted to a cup, or a coil). Removes salts, heavy metals, and pathogens. Slow and fuel-hungry. The only practical way to make seawater drinkable. See *Solar Stills* for passive versions.

## Storage

- Food-grade containers (HDPE #2, PET #1), cleaned and sanitized (1 teaspoon bleach per quart of water, swish, rinse).
- Treated municipal water: store as-is, replace every 6 months.
- Untreated water: add 1/8 teaspoon (6% bleach) per gallon before storing.
- Keep in cool, dark places away from chemicals/fuel (plastic is permeable to fumes).

## Never drink

Seawater, urine, alcohol (dehydrates), radiator fluid, water from pools treated with non-chlorine chemicals, or water from flood zones without treatment and clarification (sewage contamination is near-certain).
