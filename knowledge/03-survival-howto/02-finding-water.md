# Finding Water in the Wild

## Read the land

- Water flows downhill: check valley bottoms, drainages, and the foot of cliffs.
- Lush green vegetation (cottonwood, willow, sycamore, cattail, reeds, salt cedar) in dry country marks shallow water.
- Animal trails converging downhill often lead to water. Birds (doves, finches, pigeons) fly toward water at dawn and dusk, low and direct. Bees typically stay within a few miles of water. Mosquitoes and flies cluster near water.
- Bedrock depressions hold rain (tinajas) long after storms, especially on shaded sides.
- In dry streambeds, **dig at the outside of bends** and the lowest point; wait for seepage.
- Coastal: dig behind the first dune above high tide — fresh water floats on salt water. Taste; if salty, dig shallower/farther inland.

## Rain and dew

- Spread every tarp, poncho, and plastic sheet you have, angled into a container. Roofs collect hundreds of gallons per storm (see *Rainwater Catchment*). Discard the first few minutes of runoff.
- Dew: tie absorbent cloth around your ankles and walk through grass before sunrise; wring out. Wipe dew from non-toxic surfaces.

## Plants

- **Transpiration bag:** tie a clear plastic bag over a leafy, non-toxic branch in sun; water condenses within hours (a cup or so per day). Place a small rock in the low corner.
- **Grapevines and some vines:** cut the vine high, then low; clear sap that drips is drinkable if it is not milky, bitter, or sticky.
- **Coconuts** (green): safe and abundant water.
- **Cacti:** NOT a reliable source; most cactus pulp causes vomiting.
- **Bamboo** sections sometimes hold water (shake to listen).

## Snow and ice

- Melt before consuming — eating snow drains body heat and causes mouth injury.
- Ice yields more water per volume and fuel than snow.
- Add a little water to the pot before snow to avoid scorching.
- Old sea ice (bluish, rounded, brittle) is low in salt; new sea ice (gray, milky) is salty.

## Solar still (low yield)

Dig a pit about 3 ft wide and 2 ft deep in damp soil in full sun; place a container in the center; fill around it with green non-toxic plants or brackish water/urine-soaked soil; cover with clear plastic sealed with dirt; put a small stone in the center so the plastic dips over the container. Yield is low (often under 1 liter/day) — it may cost more sweat to dig than it produces in hot deserts. Better as a distiller for contaminated water.

## Conserve water you have

- Ration sweat, not water: rest in shade during heat, work at night, stay covered, don't talk or smoke, breathe through your nose.
- Do not eat much if water is very short (digestion uses water), especially protein.
- Drink what you need; dehydration impairs judgment. Water in your canteen doesn't help if you collapse.
