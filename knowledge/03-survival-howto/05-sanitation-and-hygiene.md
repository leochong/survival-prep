# Sanitation, Hygiene, and Disease Prevention

Poor sanitation turns disasters into epidemics (cholera, dysentery, typhoid, hepatitis A). It's unglamorous — and it saves more lives than almost anything else.

## Handwashing

- Soap and water, 20 seconds, before food prep/eating and after toilet, caring for the sick, or handling waste.
- **Tippy tap:** hang a jug with a small hole near the cap from a stick; a foot pedal on a string tips it. Uses very little water.
- No soap: ash or sand with water works as an abrasive cleanser.
- Hand sanitizer must be at least 60% alcohol.

## Toilets

### Two-bucket toilet (indoor, short-term)
- Bucket 1 for urine (much lower disease risk; dilute 1:10 and use as fertilizer on non-food plants, or dump away from water).
- Bucket 2 for feces, lined with a heavy bag; cover each use with sawdust, ash, dry leaves, or cat litter. Tight lid. Keeping waste dry dramatically cuts smell and flies.
- Toilet seat on top; keep a bleach solution nearby.

### Cat hole (traveling)
6–8 inches deep, at least 200 feet (70 big steps) from water, trails, and camp. Cover and disguise.

### Trench / pit latrine (longer-term group)
- At least 200 ft from any water source and **downhill/down-gradient** from wells; at least 5 ft above the water table.
- Pit 1–2 ft wide, 3–6 ft deep. Cover each use with soil/ash. Close when filled to 1 ft from the top; mound soil over.
- Build a superstructure for privacy and a lid to keep flies out. Ventilated improved pit (VIP) design: a vent pipe with fly screen draws odors upward.
- Roughly one latrine per 20 people.

## Greywater and garbage

- Dispose of dishwater 200 ft from water sources into a soakage pit filled with rocks.
- Burn or bury garbage away from camp; burn plastics only if unavoidable (toxic smoke). Keep food waste from attracting rats, bears, and flies.

## Bleach solutions (with ~6% household bleach)

- **Surface/dish sanitizing (≈200 ppm):** 1 tablespoon per gallon of water.
- **Disinfecting contaminated surfaces, waste buckets, sickroom (≈0.1–0.5%):** 1/3 cup to 1 cup per gallon. Make fresh daily.
- Clean off dirt first; leave surfaces wet 1+ minute.
- **Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or acids** (toxic chloramine or chlorine gas).

## Personal hygiene

- Wash body daily with a damp cloth if water is short; prioritize hands, feet, armpits, groin.
- Keep feet dry; change socks.
- Brush teeth (see *Dental Emergencies*).
- Menstrual care: reusable cloth pads (washed with soap, sun-dried), menstrual cups (boil between cycles).
- Lice/scabies spread in crowded conditions: wash bedding in hot water or seal it in bags for 2 weeks; comb hair with a fine-toothed comb.

## Laundry

Plunger in a bucket (a clean toilet plunger works) with a little soap; wring and sun-dry (UV helps disinfect). Boil or bleach the clothing and bedding of sick people.

## Caring for the sick at home

Designate one caregiver and an isolation area; ventilate; mask both patient and caregiver if respiratory; separate dishes; disinfect waste.

## The dead

Bodies of people who died from trauma pose little epidemic risk — the bigger risk is to water supplies and morale. Those who died of cholera, Ebola, or similar infections require gloves and careful handling. Bury at least 100 ft (30 m) from water sources, at least 5 ft above the water table, and record names and locations for families.
