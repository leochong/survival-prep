# Signaling for Rescue

## Signals in threes

Three of anything is a recognized distress signal: three whistle blasts, three fires in a triangle or line, three gunshots, three flashes.

## Whistle

Carries much farther than a voice and costs no energy. Three long blasts, rest, repeat.

## Signal mirror

Visible 10+ miles in sunshine.
1. Extend one arm, make a V with two fingers, and put the target (aircraft, ship, ridge) in the V.
2. Hold the mirror near your eye and tilt it until the reflected spot of light falls on your fingers, then rock it slightly to flash the target.
3. Improvise with a CD, polished metal, phone screen, or foil.

## Signal fires and smoke

- Prepare three fires in a triangle about 100 ft apart in an open area, ready to light.
- **Day:** white smoke (add green leaves, moss, damp grass) against dark backgrounds; black smoke (rubber, oil) against snow or light backgrounds.
- **Night:** bright flame.
- A burning tree (isolated conifer) makes a big signal — only where it won't start a wildfire.

## Ground-to-air signals

Make symbols at least 10 ft long and 3 ft wide, contrasting with the background (logs on snow, stamped snow, rocks, clothing, trenches with shadows).

- **V** — require assistance
- **X** — require medical assistance
- **N** — no/negative
- **Y** — yes/affirmative
- **↑ (arrow)** — proceeding in this direction
- **SOS** — universal distress

## Lights and electronics

- Strobe/flashlight: SOS in Morse — three short, three long, three short (· · · — — — · · ·).
- **Personal locator beacon (PLB) or satellite messenger:** the most reliable rescue tool when satellites and rescue services exist. Register PLBs with NOAA.
- Cell phones: text often gets through when calls fail. Climb high for signal. Save battery with airplane mode between attempts.

## Body signals to aircraft

Both arms raised in a Y = "yes / need help." One arm up, one down (diagonal) = "no / don't need help."

## Making yourself visible

Stay in the open near your signals; wear or lay out bright clothing. If lost, **stay put** (hug a tree) once you know people will search — moving targets are much harder to find.
