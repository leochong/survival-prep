# Nuclear Fallout, Radiation, and Chemical Emergencies

## Nuclear detonation: immediate actions

1. **Flash:** drop and cover behind something solid; do not look at the flash. Stay down until the blast wave passes (can take 30+ seconds at distance).
2. **Get inside within 10 minutes** — before fallout (sand-like grit) begins falling, typically 15+ minutes after detonation downwind.
3. Best shelter: the **basement or center of a large brick/concrete building**, away from windows and roof. Every layer of mass between you and the outside reduces dose. Cars offer almost no protection.
4. **Stay inside at least 24 hours** unless threatened by fire/collapse or directed by authorities. Radiation from fallout drops very rapidly.

## The 7–10 rule

For every **7-fold increase in time** after detonation, fallout radiation drops by about **10 times**. Example: dose rate at hour 1 = 100 units → hour 7 = 10 → hour 49 (2 days) = 1 → 2 weeks ≈ 0.1.

## Protection factors (approximate)

- Wood-frame house, ground floor: 2–3× reduction
- Basement of a house: ~10×
- Center of a multi-story concrete building (middle floors): 10–200×
- Expedient trench shelter covered with 3 ft of earth: 200+×

Shielding halves gamma radiation per: about 2.4 in (6 cm) packed earth, 1.8 in (4.5 cm) concrete, 3.6 in (9 cm) wood (half-value thickness approximations, after Kearny). Ten halvings ≈ 1000× reduction.

## Decontamination

- Before entering the shelter (if exposed): remove outer clothing (removes up to 90% of contamination), bag it, place away from people.
- Shower with soap and shampoo (no conditioner — it binds particles); if no shower, wipe exposed skin with a damp cloth. Gently blow nose, wipe eyelids and ears.
- Packaged food and bottled water in closed containers are safe; wipe containers before opening.
- Pets: wash with soap and water.

## Potassium iodide (KI)

- Only protects the **thyroid** against radioactive iodine. It does not protect against external radiation or other isotopes.
- Take only when public-health authorities advise or when exposure to radioiodine (reactor accident or fallout plume) is likely.
- FDA dosing: adults 130 mg; children 3–18 years 65 mg (adult-sized teens 130 mg); 1 month–3 years 32 mg; newborn to 1 month 16 mg. One dose protects ~24 hours.
- Do **not** use iodine tincture, iodized salt, or povidone-iodine as a substitute — they are ineffective and/or toxic.
- Adults over 40 benefit little and have higher side-effect risk unless exposure is very high.

## Radiation sickness (rough guide)

Nausea and vomiting within hours of exposure suggest a significant dose; the sooner vomiting begins, the higher the dose. Supportive care: hydration, hygiene, infection prevention (immune system suppressed for weeks), nutrition, rest.

## After 24–48 hours

- Short trips out may be possible as dose rates fall; follow official guidance or a dosimeter/survey meter (stock one if you can — or build a Kearny Fallout Meter from published plans).
- Avoid areas with heavy visible fallout; walk upwind/crosswind of the plume.
- Do not eat garden produce or milk from animals exposed to fallout in the first weeks without washing/peeling and, ideally, testing.

## Chemical releases

- Shelter in place: go indoors, close windows/doors, turn off HVAC, move to an upper interior room (many toxic gases are heavier than air), seal gaps with plastic and tape.
- If outdoors: move **upwind and uphill**, perpendicular to the wind.
- Decontaminate: remove clothing, flush skin and eyes with lots of water.
- Chlorine: bleach smell, coughing, burning eyes. Ammonia: sharp smell. Seek fresh air immediately.
