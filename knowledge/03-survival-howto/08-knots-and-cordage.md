# Knots and Natural Cordage

## Seven essential knots

1. **Square (reef) knot:** join two ends of the same rope (bandages, bundles). "Right over left, left over right." Not for critical loads.
2. **Bowline:** a fixed loop that won't slip or jam — rescue loop, tying to trees, hanging bags. "The rabbit comes out of the hole, around the tree, and back down the hole."
3. **Clove hitch:** quick attachment to a pole; start of lashings.
4. **Taut-line hitch:** adjustable loop for tent and tarp guylines.
5. **Trucker's hitch:** mechanical advantage (about 3:1) for tight ridgelines and securing loads.
6. **Sheet bend:** joins two ropes of different thickness.
7. **Prusik knot:** friction hitch that grips a thicker rope when loaded and slides when not — climbing and hauling.

Also useful: **figure-eight** (stopper knot at rope ends), **two half hitches** (quick tie-off), **timber hitch** (dragging logs).

## Lashings

- **Square lashing:** joins poles at right angles (clove hitch start, 3–4 wraps, 2–3 frapping turns, clove hitch finish).
- **Diagonal lashing:** poles crossing at an angle that tend to spring apart (timber hitch start).
- **Shear/tripod lashing:** wrap 3 poles side by side loosely, frap between, then spread into a tripod.

## Natural cordage

### Good fiber sources (US)
- **Inner bark:** basswood/linden, willow, cedar (western redcedar), elm, mulberry, tulip poplar, cottonwood. Soak or ret (rot in water for weeks) for best fibers.
- **Plant stalks (dead, fall/winter):** dogbane (best North American cordage plant), milkweed, stinging nettle, fireweed, hemp.
- **Leaves:** yucca and agave — scrape or pound to free fibers.
- **Animal:** sinew (back strap and leg tendons of deer), rawhide strips.
- **Roots:** spruce roots split for lashing.

### Reverse-wrap two-ply cordage
1. Take a bundle of fibers and twist it in the middle until it kinks into a loop.
2. Hold the kink. Twist the top strand **away** from you (clockwise), then cross it **toward** you over the bottom strand (counterclockwise).
3. Repeat: twist away, wrap toward. The opposing twists lock the cord.
4. Splice in new fibers by staggering the joins so both strands don't end at once.
A skilled maker produces a few feet per hour; strong enough for snares, bow drills, fishing line, and lashings.

### Quick emergency cordage
Strips of clothing, tarps, plastic bags (twisted), electrical wire, paracord inner strands (7 strands in 550 cord), vehicle seat belts, vines (greenbrier, grapevine, Virginia creeper) for rough lashings.
