# US Regions — Terrain, Climate, and Natural Hazards

Knowing your region's dominant hazards shapes your shelter, water, and evacuation plans. Regional plant guides live in `02-plants-and-herbal/regions/`.

## Northeast
- **Terrain:** Appalachian and Adirondack mountains, glacial lakes, dense forest, dense urban corridor (Boston–DC).
- **Climate:** cold snowy winters, humid summers, reliable rainfall.
- **Hazards:** nor'easters and blizzards, ice storms (long power outages), hurricanes along the coast, river flooding, urban density during evacuations.
- **Water:** abundant surface water; urban runoff contamination.

## Southeast
- **Terrain:** coastal plain, Piedmont, southern Appalachians, swamps, barrier islands, Florida's flat limestone (karst, sinkholes).
- **Climate:** humid subtropical; hot humid summers; mild winters.
- **Hazards:** hurricanes (June–November) with storm surge as the leading killer, tornadoes (Dixie Alley, often at night), flooding, extreme heat, lightning.
- **Water:** abundant but biologically contaminated; shallow aquifers.

## Midwest
- **Terrain:** flat to rolling farmland, Great Lakes, river systems (Mississippi, Ohio, Missouri).
- **Climate:** continental — severe cold winters, hot humid summers.
- **Hazards:** tornadoes, blizzards and extreme cold, river floods, derechos, the New Madrid Seismic Zone (southeast Missouri, Tennessee, Kentucky, Arkansas, Illinois).
- **Water:** abundant; agricultural runoff (nitrates, pesticides) — boiling does not remove nitrates.

## Great Plains
- **Terrain:** wide open grassland, few trees except along rivers, gradually rising toward the Rockies.
- **Climate:** semi-arid, extreme temperature swings, constant wind.
- **Hazards:** tornadoes (Tornado Alley), blizzards with whiteouts, drought, wildfire on grasslands, hail, dust storms.
- **Water:** scarce; Ogallala aquifer wells; rivers often muddy or dry.

## Desert Southwest
- **Terrain:** basin-and-range, mesas, canyons, sky-island mountains.
- **Climate:** arid; summer highs above 110°F (43°C) in low deserts; cold nights; monsoon July–September.
- **Hazards:** extreme heat, flash floods in canyons and washes (even from distant storms), dust storms (haboobs), wildfire, water scarcity.
- **Water:** the dominant survival factor; cities depend on long-distance aqueducts and canals. **Las Vegas** (Mojave) gets about 90% of its water from Lake Mead, and summer highs exceed 110°F.

## Great Basin
- **Area:** most of Nevada (Reno, Carson City, Elko), western Utah, southeastern Oregon, and the eastern edge of California.
- **Terrain:** north–south mountain ranges separated by wide sagebrush valleys and dry lake beds (playas). No rivers drain to the sea; water ends in sinks and salty lakes (Pyramid Lake, Great Salt Lake).
- **Climate:** high cold desert, with hot dry summers, cold winters, large day–night temperature swings, and 5–12 inches of precipitation.
- **Hazards:** earthquakes (the Walker Lane fault zone; Reno–Carson has a high seismic risk), wildfire in cheatgrass and sagebrush, winter storms on mountain passes (I-80 Donner Summit), drought, flash floods, extreme isolation between towns, and dust storms.
- **Water:** scarce. Rivers fed by Sierra snowmelt (Truckee, Carson, Walker), springs at the base of ranges, and wells. Many lakes and playas are salty or alkaline.

## Rocky Mountains
- **Terrain:** high mountains (to 14,000 ft), high valleys and basins.
- **Climate:** alpine and semi-arid; heavy snow; thin air.
- **Hazards:** blizzards, avalanches, lightning on peaks, wildfire, altitude illness, Yellowstone region hydrothermal hazards, isolation (long distances between towns).
- **Water:** snowmelt streams (still treat), dry in late season at lower elevations.

## Pacific Northwest
- **Terrain:** Cascade volcanoes, coastal mountains, temperate rainforest, Columbia River.
- **Climate:** wet mild winters west of the Cascades; dry, continental east of them.
- **Hazards:** **Cascadia Subduction Zone** (magnitude 9 earthquake and tsunami potential — move to high ground immediately after a long, strong coastal earthquake), volcanoes (Mount St. Helens, Rainier lahars), wildfire smoke, landslides, atmospheric-river floods.
- **Water:** abundant.

## California
- **Terrain:** coast ranges, Central Valley, Sierra Nevada, deserts.
- **Climate:** Mediterranean (wet winter, dry summer); deserts in the southeast.
- **Hazards:** earthquakes (San Andreas, Hayward faults), wildfire (worst late summer–fall with Santa Ana / Diablo winds), drought, debris flows on burn scars, tsunami on the coast, extreme heat inland.
- **Water:** heavily engineered; long-distance aqueducts vulnerable to earthquakes.

## Alaska
- **Terrain:** vast wilderness, mountains (Denali), tundra, glaciers, long coastline; most communities off the road system.
- **Climate:** subarctic to arctic; extreme cold Interior winters; rainforest in the Southeast.
- **Hazards:** extreme cold, earthquakes and tsunamis (1964 M9.2), volcanoes (Aleutians), avalanches, flooding, bears, extreme isolation and long supply chains.
- **Water:** abundant but freezes; glacial silt needs settling.

## Hawaii
- **Terrain:** volcanic islands, steep valleys, reefs.
- **Climate:** tropical; wet windward and dry leeward sides.
- **Hazards:** tsunamis (local and Pacific-wide), hurricanes, volcanic eruptions and vog (volcanic smog), flash floods, island isolation (most food imported).
- **Water:** rain catchment common in rural areas; streams carry leptospirosis.

## Universal hazard tips

- **Tornado:** lowest floor, interior room, no windows; cover your head. Mobile homes and vehicles are deathtraps — leave for a sturdy building or ditch.
- **Earthquake:** drop, cover, hold on. Expect aftershocks. Check gas lines (turn off only if you smell gas).
- **Flash flood:** climb to higher ground; never drive or walk into moving water (6 inches can knock you down, 12 inches can float a car).
- **Tsunami:** natural warnings — strong long quake, sudden ocean retreat, roaring sound. Go to high ground or inland immediately; don't wait for official warning. Waves come in a series for hours.
- **Wildfire:** leave early; if trapped, shelter in a large cleared area, body of water, or a building; stay low under smoke.
