# Building a Rainwater Catchment System

## How much water can you collect?

**Gallons = roof area (sq ft) × rainfall (inches) × 0.623 × efficiency (~0.8)**

Example: a 1,000 sq ft roof in 1 inch of rain → about 500 gallons. Measure the roof's **footprint** (horizontal area), not its sloped surface.

Note: rainwater harvesting is legal in most states now, but a few (notably Colorado) cap volumes; check state rules in normal times.

## Components

1. **Catchment surface:** metal roofs are best. Asphalt shingles are usable but shed grit and some chemicals — fine after treatment for drinking in an emergency. Avoid treated-wood shakes, lead flashing, or roofs near smoke stacks/trees with bird droppings.
2. **Gutters and downspouts** with leaf screens.
3. **First-flush diverter:** discards the first, dirtiest runoff (bird droppings, dust). Rule of thumb: divert about **10 gallons per 1,000 sq ft** of roof.
   - Build: a vertical 3–4 inch PVC pipe tee'd off below the downspout, capped at the bottom with a small drip hole or removable cleanout cap. When the pipe fills, the rest flows on to the tank. A floating ball can seal the top of the chamber.
4. **Storage:** food-grade, opaque (prevents algae) barrels (55-gal), IBC totes (275–330 gal, cover with paint/tarp to block light), or cisterns. Elevate on a solid level base — water weighs 8.3 lb/gal; a full 55-gal barrel is ~460 lb.
5. **Screen** inlets and overflow with fine mesh (mosquitoes breed in a week).
6. **Overflow** pipe directing excess away from foundations.
7. **Outlet** spigot several inches above the bottom so sediment stays behind.

## Linking barrels

Connect barrels near their bottoms with hose fittings so they fill and drain together, or daisy-chain overflow from one to the next.

## Treatment for drinking

Treat all collected rainwater before drinking: settle, filter, then boil or chlorinate (see *Water Purification*). Water used for gardens, washing, and toilet flushing can be used untreated.

## Maintenance

Clean gutters seasonally, empty and scrub the first-flush chamber after storms, inspect screens, and clean tanks yearly (scrub with a 1:50 bleach solution and rinse).

## Emergency improvised catchment

Tarps strung on a slant into a bucket; trash cans under downspouts; plastic sheeting on a frame. Even 100 sq ft of tarp yields 50 gallons from 1 inch of rain.
