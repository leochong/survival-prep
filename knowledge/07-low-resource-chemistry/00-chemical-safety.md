# Chemical Safety for Low-Resource Chemistry

Practical chemistry (soap, lime, disinfectants, charcoal, tanning) is invaluable — and every process has hazards. A chemical burn or poisoning without medical care can be fatal or disabling.

## Basic protective equipment

- **Eye protection** — goggles, or at minimum glasses. Caustic splashes (lye, lime) cause blindness within seconds.
- **Gloves** — rubber/nitrile for lye and acids; leather for heat.
- Long sleeves, closed shoes, an apron.
- **Ventilation** — work outdoors or with strong airflow. Stand upwind.
- Keep a large container of clean water within reach for flushing, and vinegar does **not** replace water for skin lye burns (flush with water first, for a long time).

## Never mix

| Mixture | Produces |
|---|---|
| Bleach + ammonia (incl. urine, some glass cleaners) | Chloramine gas — lung damage |
| Bleach + acids (vinegar, toilet bowl cleaner, rust remover) | Chlorine gas |
| Bleach + rubbing alcohol or acetone | Chloroform and other toxic compounds |
| Hydrogen peroxide + vinegar (stored together) | Peracetic acid — corrosive |
| Pool shock (calcium hypochlorite) + organics, fuel, or brake fluid | Fire / violent reaction |
| Different drain cleaners together | Violent heat, splattering |

## Golden rules

1. **Add strong chemicals to water**, slowly, stirring — not water onto lye or acid (it can boil and spatter).
2. Label every container clearly with contents and date. **Never store chemicals in food or drink containers** — poisonings of children are common this way.
3. Store acids away from bases, oxidizers (bleach, pool shock) away from fuels and organics.
4. Keep chemicals locked up and out of reach of children.
5. Use dedicated equipment (pots, spoons) for chemical work — never reuse for food. Aluminum reacts violently with lye; use stainless steel, enamel, glass, or HDPE plastic.
6. Do small test batches first.
7. Burning plastics, treated lumber, and painted wood releases toxic fumes — don't.

## Measuring pH without a meter

- **Red cabbage indicator:** boil chopped red cabbage, strain. Purple = neutral; red/pink = acid; green/blue = basic; yellow = strongly basic.
- Turmeric paper turns red-brown in bases.
- The "tongue test" for soap/lye — don't. Use indicators.

## First aid for chemical exposure

- **Skin:** brush off dry powder (lime, lye flakes) first, remove contaminated clothing, flush with running water for 20+ minutes (caustics: 60 minutes).
- **Eyes:** flush immediately with clean water for at least 20 minutes, holding lids open; tilt so runoff doesn't enter the other eye.
- **Inhaled gas:** move to fresh air; rest upright; watch for delayed lung swelling (breathlessness hours later).
- **Swallowed caustics:** do **not** induce vomiting; rinse mouth; small sips of water or milk if able to swallow.
- See *Burn Treatment* for chemical burns.
