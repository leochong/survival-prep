# Making Charcoal and Activated Charcoal

Charcoal burns hotter and cleaner than wood (needed for forges and smelting), filters water, deodorizes, makes biochar for soil, and in activated form treats some poisonings.

## Principle

Heat wood **without enough oxygen** to burn it (pyrolysis). Water, then volatile gases and tars, are driven off, leaving mostly carbon. Yield is about 15–30% of the original wood weight.

## Method 1: Retort (small batches, best quality)

1. Fill a metal can or drum (with a lid) with dry hardwood pieces, packed tightly.
2. Punch a small hole (nail-sized for a small can, finger-sized for a drum) in the lid.
3. Place it in or over a hot fire.
4. Gas jets out of the hole and ignites. When the jets stop (30 minutes to several hours depending on size), remove from the fire.
5. Plug the hole with clay/mud and **let it cool completely** (hours) before opening — exposing hot charcoal to air re-ignites it.

The **TLUD (top-lit updraft) gasifier** and the **"two-barrel" retort** (a smaller sealed barrel inside a larger burning barrel) are efficient larger designs — the escaping gases fuel the outer fire.

## Method 2: Earth pit / mound kiln (large batches)

1. Dig a pit or stack wood in a tight dome.
2. Light from the top or a central chimney; once burning well, cover with leaves/grass and then 4–6 inches of soil, leaving small vents.
3. Watch the smoke: thick white (steam) → yellowish (tar) → thin blue/clear (nearly done). Close vents progressively.
4. Seal completely and let cool for 1–3 days before opening.
Lower yield and quality than retorts; produces significant smoke.

## Activated charcoal

Activation creates enormous internal surface area that adsorbs chemicals, toxins, odors, and colors.

### Simple activation
1. Grind charcoal to small granules (finely, if for medical use).
2. **Chemical method:** soak in a 25% solution of calcium chloride or in strong lemon juice for 24 hours. Drain.
3. Heat in a covered pot (or a retort) to very high heat (red heat if possible) for about an hour, without air entering.
4. Rinse with clean water and dry. Store sealed.
Steam activation (passing steam through red-hot charcoal) is what industry uses and is achievable with improvised setups, but the chemical method is more accessible.

Homemade activated charcoal is less effective than pharmaceutical grade but still useful for water filters and odor control.

## Medical use (activated charcoal)

- For some swallowed poisons within about 1 hour: adults ~50 g in water; children ~1 g/kg (max 50 g).
- **Not effective for:** alcohols, acids, alkalis (lye), iron, lithium, petroleum products. Never give to someone drowsy, seizing, or without a gag reflex (aspiration risk).
- Homemade charcoal must be pure hardwood charcoal (no briquettes — they contain additives).

## Biochar

Crush charcoal and "charge" it by soaking in compost tea, urine (diluted), or mixing into compost for a few weeks before adding to garden soil — fresh charcoal initially pulls nutrients from soil. Improves water retention and soil life long-term.
