# Disinfectants and Antiseptics from Low Resources

## Chlorine bleach dilutions (from 6% sodium hypochlorite)

| Purpose | Strength | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking water | ~2 ppm | 8 drops per gallon (see *Water Purification*) |
| Dishes, food surfaces (then air dry) | ~200 ppm | 1 tablespoon per gallon |
| General disinfection, toilets, sickrooms | ~0.1% (1,000 ppm) | 1/3 cup per gallon |
| Blood/body fluid spills, cholera/Ebola settings | 0.5% | ~1 cup per gallon (1:10) |
| Hand-washing in outbreak settings (WHO, short-term) | 0.05% | 1 tablespoon per liter |

- Make fresh solutions daily; chlorine degrades in light and heat.
- Clean visible dirt first. Contact time: at least 1 minute (10 minutes for heavy contamination).
- Store bleach cool and dark; buy fresh periodically or store **pool shock** (calcium hypochlorite) for long-term use (see *Water Purification* for preparation).

## Dakin's solution (wound irrigation, diluted hypochlorite)

A historic WWI wound antiseptic, still used today. **Quarter-strength (0.125%)** is common for wound care:
1. Boil 1 quart (≈ 1 liter) of water for 15 minutes; cool covered.
2. Add **½ teaspoon baking soda** (buffers the pH, reducing tissue irritation).
3. Add about **1½ tablespoons (≈ 21 mL)** of 6% unscented bleach for quarter-strength (~0.125%), or **3 tablespoons (≈ 42 mL)** for half-strength (~0.25%). With 8.25% bleach use about three-quarters of those amounts. Old bleach is weaker — these are approximations.
4. Store in a sealed, clean, opaque jar; discard after 48 hours. Use only on skin/wounds, never on eyes or mucous membranes, and stop if the tissue looks irritated. Plain clean water irrigation is safe for most wounds.

## Alcohol

- **60–90% ethanol or isopropyl** is an effective skin antiseptic and surface disinfectant; **70%** is the optimal sweet spot (water helps penetrate cells).
- 80-proof liquor (40%) is not an effective disinfectant.
- **WHO hand-rub formulation (1 liter):** 833 mL of 96% ethanol (or 751 mL of 99.8% isopropyl), 42 mL 3% hydrogen peroxide, 15 mL 98% glycerol, top up to 1 liter with boiled cooled water. Let stand 72 hours before use to kill any spores introduced.
- Highly flammable — store away from flames.
- Home distillation of alcohol is illegal under US federal law without a permit in normal times, and it carries fire and methanol hazards: discard the first portion of distillate (the "heads"), which concentrates methanol.

## Hydrogen peroxide

3% solution: surface disinfectant (leave 1+ minute), mouth rinse (diluted 1:1, do not swallow). Repeated use in wounds damages healing tissue. Decomposes in light — store in an opaque bottle.

## Saline

Normal saline (0.9%) for wound irrigation, eye rinsing, and nasal rinsing: **1 teaspoon (≈ 5 g) non-iodized salt per 2 cups (≈ 500 mL) boiled water**, cooled. For nasal rinse, use only boiled or distilled water (amoeba risk with untreated tap water).

## Vinegar

5% acetic acid kills some bacteria and is a decent cleaner, but it is **not a reliable disinfectant** against many pathogens. Useful for removing mineral deposits and as a mild antiseptic soak for fungal skin infections (dilute 1:1). Never combine with bleach.

## Heat and sunlight

- **Boiling** 20 minutes: instruments, cloth, bottles.
- **Pressure cooker** (15 psi, 30 min): closest home equivalent to an autoclave.
- **Dry heat** (oven 340°F / 170°C for 1 hour): metal instruments and glass.
- **Sunlight:** UV plus drying helps disinfect bedding and clothing; not sterilizing.

## Honey and sugar

Honey, and granulated sugar packed into wounds ("sugar paste"), draw moisture and inhibit bacteria — historically and currently used in low-resource wound care. See *Wound Care*.
