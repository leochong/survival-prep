# Food Preservation Without Refrigeration

Preservation works by removing something microbes need: water (drying, salting, sugar), warmth (cold storage), oxygen (canning, fat-sealing), or neutral pH (pickling, fermenting) — or by killing them with heat (canning).

## Drying (dehydrating)

The simplest and most fuel-efficient method.
- **Sun drying:** fruits in hot, dry, sunny climates (Southwest, interior West) — 85°F+, low humidity. Slice thin, lay on screens, cover with cheesecloth against insects, bring in at night (dew). 2–4 days.
- **Solar dehydrator:** a box with a black collector and screened vents, angled toward the sun — faster and cleaner than open sun.
- **Fire/oven:** lowest heat (130–140°F / 55–60°C), door ajar.
- **Done:** fruit leathery and pliable with no moisture when squeezed; vegetables brittle; jerky bends and cracks but doesn't snap.
- Store in airtight containers in cool, dark places. Pasteurize sun-dried foods against insect eggs (freeze 48 hours or heat at 160°F for 30 minutes).

## Jerky (meat drying)

- Use lean meat; trim all fat (fat goes rancid).
- Slice ¼ inch thick, with the grain for chewy, across for tender.
- **USDA safety step:** heat meat to 160°F (71°C) (poultry 165°F) before or after drying to kill pathogens — e.g., steam or roast strips briefly, or finish drying in an oven at 275°F for 10 minutes.
- Salt (and optionally a curing salt) improves safety.
- Dry at 130–140°F+ until it cracks when bent. Wild game carries parasite risk — see *Hunting and Game Processing*.

## Pemmican (long-keeping high-calorie food)

Traditional Plains food: dried lean meat pounded to powder, mixed roughly 1:1 by weight with rendered fat (tallow), optionally with dried berries (chokecherry, saskatoon). Packed into sealed containers or rawhide bags, it keeps for months to years in cool conditions. Extremely calorie-dense (~3,000+ kcal per pound).

## Salting and curing

- **Dry salting:** pack meat or fish in salt (often with sugar), stacking with salt between layers, in a cool place (36–40°F ideal). Salt draws out water. Roughly 1 lb salt per 10–12 lb of meat for country hams, cured over weeks.
- **Brining:** a brine strong enough to float an egg.
- Salt fish (cod-style): split, salt heavily, press, then air-dry.
- Nitrite curing salts (Prague Powder #1/#2) prevent botulism in cured meats — stock them and follow recipes precisely (too much is toxic).
- Desalt (soak in changes of water) before cooking.

## Smoking

- **Cold smoking** (under 90°F) flavors and partially preserves but does **not** cook — must be combined with curing/salting.
- **Hot smoking** (smoker at 225°F+, meat reaches safe internal temperature) cooks and partially preserves; store cool and eat within days to weeks unless also dried.
- Use hardwoods (hickory, oak, apple, alder, mesquite); never resinous softwoods or treated/painted wood.
- Smokehouse: a small enclosed building or barrel with the fire pit downhill connected by a buried pipe/trench so smoke arrives cool.

## Fermentation (vegetables)

**Sauerkraut / lacto-fermented vegetables:**
1. Shred cabbage; mix with salt at about **2–2.5% by weight** (≈ 3 tablespoons salt per 5 lb cabbage).
2. Pack tightly into a crock or jar until brine covers the vegetables. Weigh down so everything stays **under the brine**.
3. Ferment at 60–75°F for 3–6 weeks. White film (kahm yeast) can be skimmed; fuzzy colored mold → discard the top or the batch if extensive; slimy or putrid → discard.
Rich in vitamin C — sauerkraut historically prevented scurvy on ships.

## Canning

### Water-bath canning — HIGH-ACID foods only
Fruits, jams, pickles (with ≥ 5% vinegar), tomatoes **with added acid** (1 tablespoon bottled lemon juice per pint).
- Fill clean jars, leave headspace, wipe rims, apply lids.
- Submerge in boiling water by 1 inch; process the tested recipe time; add time at altitude.

### Pressure canning — LOW-ACID foods (required)
Vegetables, meats, fish, beans, soups, stews.
- Typical: 10 lb pressure (weighted gauge) or 11 lb (dial gauge) at sea level; increase pressure at altitude (15 lb weighted above 1,000 ft).
- Typical times: green beans 20 min (pints) / 25 min (quarts); meat 75 / 90 min. **Follow tested USDA/NCHFP recipes** — print them in advance.
- Boiling home-canned low-acid food for 10 minutes before eating destroys botulinum toxin (if present) — a sensible extra precaution. (Altitude: add 1 minute per 1,000 ft.)

### Botulism signs
Double vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, difficulty swallowing, descending paralysis, 12–72 hours after eating. Fatal without antitoxin and breathing support.

## Other methods

- **Root cellaring** (see *Root Cellars*).
- **Sugar:** jams, fruit in syrup, honey (honey keeps indefinitely).
- **Fat sealing:** confit — cooked meat covered with rendered fat in crocks, kept cool.
- **Water glassing eggs:** unwashed, clean fresh eggs submerged in a solution of food-grade hydrated lime (about 1 oz lime per quart of water) keep 8–12 months in a cool place. Never use cracked or washed eggs.
