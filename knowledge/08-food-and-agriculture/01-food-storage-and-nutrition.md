# Long-Term Food Storage and Survival Nutrition

## Calorie needs

- Sedentary adult: 1,800–2,400 kcal/day; heavy labor or cold weather: 3,000–5,000+.
- Children, pregnant and breastfeeding people have higher relative needs — prioritize them.
- Rough rule: **1 lb of dry grains/beans ≈ 1,600 kcal.** A year's basic supply for one adult is about **300 lb grains, 60 lb beans/legumes, 20 lb fats/oils, 60 lb sugar/honey, 8 lb salt**, plus powdered milk and multivitamins (LDS/USU "basic four" style planning).

## Best long-term staples (properly packaged, cool and dry)

| Food | Shelf life (sealed, < 70°F) |
|---|---|
| White rice | 25–30 years |
| Hard wheat berries | 25–30+ years |
| Dry beans, lentils, split peas | 20–30 years (older beans take longer to cook) |
| Rolled oats | 20–30 years |
| Pasta | 20+ years |
| Sugar, honey, salt | Indefinite |
| Powdered milk (nonfat) | ~20 years |
| Brown rice, whole-grain flour, nuts | 6–12 months (oils go rancid) |
| Cooking oil | 1–2 years (store cool and dark) |
| Canned goods | 2–5+ years (quality declines; safe if can is intact) |

### Packaging
- Food-grade buckets with **Mylar bags** and **oxygen absorbers** (about 300–500 cc per gallon), sealed with an iron/hair straightener.
- Do not use oxygen absorbers with sugar or salt (they become rock-hard).
- Store off the floor, away from light, heat, and rodents. Temperature matters most: every ~18°F (10°C) rise halves shelf life.
- Freeze grains 72 hours (or use oxygen absorbers) to kill insect eggs.
- **Rotate:** "store what you eat, eat what you store."

## Micronutrient deficiencies to prevent

- **Scurvy (vitamin C):** bleeding gums, bruising, poor wound healing, joint pain after 1–3 months without vitamin C. Sources: fresh/fermented vegetables, sprouts, rose hips, pine/spruce needle tea, citrus, potatoes, organ meats, sumac.
- **Pellagra (niacin):** if eating a corn-heavy diet — nixtamalize corn (see *Lime*); eat beans, meat, peanuts.
- **Beriberi (thiamine/B1):** from diets of mostly white rice — add beans, whole grains, meat.
- **Night blindness (vitamin A):** orange vegetables, dark leafy greens, liver, eggs.
- **Iodine deficiency (goiter):** iodized salt (store it), seaweed, seafood.
- **Protein-energy malnutrition** in children: watch for wasting, swelling of legs/belly (kwashiorkor).

### Sprouting
Sprouting stored seeds (wheat, lentils, mung beans, alfalfa) for 2–5 days in a jar, rinsing twice daily, creates vitamin C and B vitamins from dry storage foods. Use clean, treated water.

## "Rabbit starvation" (protein poisoning)

Eating mostly lean meat (rabbit, lean game) without fat or carbohydrates causes nausea, diarrhea, and eventual death within weeks. Protein should not exceed ~35% of calories; add fat (marrow, organ fat, tallow, nuts, oils) or carbohydrates.

## Rationing

- Start rationing at the beginning, not when supplies are low.
- Don't drastically cut food if water is plentiful and you are working hard — weakness causes injuries and bad decisions.
- Eat perishables first, then pantry foods, then long-term storage.
- If water is scarce, reduce protein and salty foods.

## Cooking stored staples

- Soak beans overnight; discard soak water; boil (kidney beans hard boil 10 minutes+). A pressure cooker saves enormous fuel.
- Grind wheat with a hand grain mill (store one). Whole wheat can also be cooked whole as a porridge ("cracked wheat") or sprouted.
- Rice and beans together form a complete protein.
