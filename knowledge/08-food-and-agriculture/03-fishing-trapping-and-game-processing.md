# Fishing, Trapping, and Processing Game

Wild protein is valuable, but hunting and fishing pressure after a major disaster will rapidly deplete game near populated areas. Fishing and trapping are generally more energy-efficient than active hunting.

## Fishing

### Gear to stock
Hooks in many sizes, monofilament line, sinkers, bobbers, swivels, a small tackle kit, a cast net, a collapsible minnow trap. Passive gear (trotlines, limb lines, traps) works while you do other tasks.

### Improvised gear
- **Hooks:** safety pins, thorn (hawthorn), bone, carved wood gorge (a sliver sharpened at both ends, tied at the middle, swallowed lengthwise with bait).
- **Line:** inner strands of paracord, thread, natural cordage.
- **Bait:** worms, grubs, insects, crayfish, small fish, bits of guts.

### Methods
- **Trotline/set lines:** a main line across a stream or along the bank with multiple baited droppers. Check every few hours.
- **Fish trap (funnel weir):** stones or stakes forming a V pointing downstream into a holding pen; fish swim in and struggle to find the narrow exit.
- **Bottle trap:** cut the top off a plastic bottle, invert it into the bottom section, bait inside.
- **Spearfishing:** a forked spear sharpened, best at night with a light in shallow water. Aim below the fish (refraction).
- Fish feed most at dawn and dusk; look for deeper holes, undercut banks, eddies behind rocks, and where streams enter lakes.

### Safety
Avoid fish from polluted water. Check for state fish-consumption advisories (mercury, PCBs) while available. Cook freshwater fish thoroughly (parasites). Spoiled fish: sunken eyes, slimy gills, ammonia smell, flesh that stays dented.

## Trapping (small game)

Snares and deadfalls let you "hunt" 24 hours a day.
- **Locations:** game trails, runways under brush, den entrances, water crossings, fence gaps. Look for tracks, droppings, and chewed plants.
- **Snare:** a loop of wire (picture-hanging or snare wire) or strong cord about fist-size for rabbits, set about 4 fingers above the trail, anchored firmly, with sticks funneling the animal into it.
- **Figure-4 deadfall and Paiute deadfall:** a heavy flat rock or log propped with a trigger mechanism that falls when bait is disturbed.
- Set many traps (10–20+); success rates per trap are low. Check them at least daily — humane practice and prevents losing catch to scavengers.
- Avoid human scent where possible (smoke your hands and wire).
- In normal times, many trap types are restricted or illegal — know the law.

## Field dressing and processing

1. Make sure the animal is dead. Wear gloves when possible.
2. **Bleed** (cut the throat or large vessels of the neck/chest) promptly.
3. **Gut:** lay on its back, cut a shallow line from the pelvis to the sternum without puncturing the gut (two fingers under the knife lift the skin). Cut around the anus, remove the entrails and organs. Save heart, liver (if healthy-looking — no spots, worms, or cysts), and kidneys.
4. **Cool the carcass quickly:** prop the cavity open, hang in shade. Spoilage begins quickly in warm weather.
5. **Skin and butcher:** for rabbits and squirrels, a small cut across the back lets you pull the skin off in two directions. For deer, hang from the hind legs, skin downward, quarter the carcass.
6. Use everything: hides (see *Tanning*), sinew (cordage), bones (marrow, broth, tools), fat (rendering), brains (tanning).

## Disease precautions

- **Tularemia (rabbits, hares, rodents):** wear gloves, avoid sick or sluggish animals (especially in summer), cook thoroughly. Signs in the liver: white spots.
- **Trichinosis:** bear, wild boar, cougar, walrus — cook to 160°F (71°C). Freezing does not reliably kill trichinella in wild game.
- **Chronic wasting disease (deer, elk, moose):** prion disease spreading in many states. Avoid animals that look sick/emaciated; do not eat brain, spinal cord, spleen, or lymph nodes; bone out meat.
- **Rabies:** never eat animals behaving abnormally.
- **Brucellosis (wild hogs, bison):** wear gloves and eye protection when dressing; cook thoroughly.
- **Raccoon roundworm:** wash hands; cook raccoon thoroughly; avoid feces.
- **Birds:** avoid sick/dead birds (avian influenza). Cook poultry to 165°F.

## Insects (overlooked protein)

Grasshoppers, crickets, grubs (beetle larvae), termites, and ants are nutritious. Cook all insects (parasites); remove legs and wings from grasshoppers. Avoid brightly colored insects, hairy caterpillars, and anything that smells foul or bites/stings.
