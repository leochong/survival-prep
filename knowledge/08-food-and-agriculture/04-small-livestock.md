# Raising Small Livestock

Small animals convert scraps, forage, and weeds into eggs, meat, milk, and fertilizer with modest space.

## Chickens

- **Output:** 4–6 eggs/week per hen from heritage breeds in their first 2–3 years; production drops in winter (less light) and during molt.
- **Hardy dual-purpose breeds:** Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock (Barred Rock), Australorp, Orpington, Wyandotte, Delaware. They go broody and raise chicks (a must for sustainability — keep a rooster).
- **Housing:** dry, draft-free but ventilated coop, ~4 sq ft per bird inside and 10 sq ft in a run; roosts and nest boxes (1 per 4 hens). Predator-proof with hardware cloth (not chicken wire) buried 12 inches or with an apron.
- **Feed:** ~¼ lb per day per bird of balanced feed. Supplement/replace with free range, kitchen scraps (no moldy food, avocado, or raw dry beans), sprouted grains, black soldier fly larvae, garden waste. Oyster shell or crushed eggshells for calcium.
- **Water:** always available, clean; heated or rotated in winter.
- **Predators:** raccoons, foxes, coyotes, hawks, owls, weasels, dogs. Lock up at night.

## Ducks

Hardier than chickens in wet climates, excellent slug control, good layers (Khaki Campbell, Welsh Harlequin). Need water for dunking heads (not a pond).

## Rabbits

- One doe can produce 30–50+ kits/year (4–6 litters), reaching butcher weight (~5 lb) in 10–12 weeks. Very efficient meat.
- **Breeds:** New Zealand, Californian, Champagne d'Argent.
- **Housing:** hutches or colony pens, shade (rabbits die of heat above ~85°F — provide frozen water bottles), protection from predators.
- **Feed:** hay plus pellets; can be raised on grass hay, forage (clover, dandelion, plantain), and garden scraps — introduce new greens slowly. Avoid toxic plants (nightshades, milkweed, rhubarb leaves).
- **Nutrition caution:** rabbit meat is extremely lean — eat with fats (see *rabbit starvation* in *Food Storage and Nutrition*).
- Rabbit manure can go directly onto gardens (no composting needed).

## Goats

- **Dairy goats** (Nubian, Alpine, LaMancha, Nigerian Dwarf) produce 1–3 quarts/day (Nigerian Dwarf less but richer). Must be bred yearly to keep milking.
- Browsers: eat brush, weeds, tree leaves. Need strong fencing (they escape everything) and a dry shelter.
- **Toxic plants** for goats: rhododendron/azalea, mountain laurel, wild cherry wilted leaves, yew, oleander.
- **Health:** monitor for worms (check inner eyelids — pale = anemic from barber pole worm; FAMACHA system), trim hooves every 6–8 weeks.
- **Milk safety:** pasteurize by heating to 161°F for 15 seconds or 145°F for 30 minutes, then chill — prevents brucellosis, TB, listeria, E. coli.

## Bees

- Honey (keeps indefinitely, wound dressing), wax (candles, salves, waterproofing), and pollination that boosts garden yields.
- A hive can yield 30–60+ lb of surplus honey per year in good areas.
- Start with 2 hives (compare and share resources). Langstroth or top-bar hives.
- Leave enough honey for winter (60–90 lb in northern climates).
- Have an epinephrine auto-injector if anyone is allergic.

## Other options

Guinea fowl (tick control, alarm system), quail (small space, fast), pigs (convert scraps, need lots of feed), sheep (wool and meat, grazing), fish in ponds or barrels (tilapia in warm areas, catfish), worms (vermicompost and chicken feed).

## Basic husbandry rules

- Clean water daily, dry shelter, protection from predators.
- Quarantine new animals 30 days.
- Butcher humanely, cool meat promptly.
- Plan winter feed — stored hay, grain, roots — before fall.
