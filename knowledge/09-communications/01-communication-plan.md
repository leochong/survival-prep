# Family and Group Communication Plan

Disasters usually strike when family members are scattered (work, school). Decide in advance how you will find each other.

## PACE plan

Have four layers, used in order:
- **P — Primary:** cell phone call/text. (Texts get through congested networks more often than calls.)
- **A — Alternate:** out-of-state contact person everyone texts/calls (long-distance lines are often less congested than local ones), messaging apps.
- **C — Contingency:** GMRS/ham radio at scheduled times and channels.
- **E — Emergency:** go to a pre-agreed rally point; leave written messages.

## Rally points

1. **Near home:** a spot outside the house (for fire).
2. **Neighborhood:** a landmark nearby if you can't return home.
3. **Out of area:** a relative's home or other location in another town, reached by a planned route.

Write them down for everyone, with routes, on a waterproof card. Include school pickup arrangements.

## Radio check-in schedule

Agree on times and channels, e.g., "GMRS channel 17, privacy code off, at the top of every odd hour for 5 minutes" or "146.520 simplex at 0800 and 2000." Conserve batteries by turning radios on only at schedule times when power is short.

## Message board / dead drop

At the home or rally point, leave dated, timed notes: who, status, where going, when. Use a pre-agreed hidden location if security is a concern.

## Essential contact card (for every person)

Names and numbers (written — no one memorizes numbers anymore), out-of-state contact, medical conditions and medications, allergies, blood type, rally points, radio channels/frequencies, meeting times.

## NATO phonetic alphabet

Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.
Numbers: "niner" for 9, "fife" for 5 on poor connections.

## Prowords

- "Over" — I'm done talking, your turn. "Out" — conversation ended (never "over and out").
- "Say again" — repeat. "Roger" — received. "Wilco" — will comply. "Break" — pause/emergency interruption. "Mayday" (×3) — life-threatening emergency. "Pan-pan" (×3) — urgent, not life-threatening.

## Operational security

Radio transmissions are public. In unstable situations, avoid broadcasting your supplies, location, or group size. Use agreed code words for places and status (e.g., "blue" = all safe).
